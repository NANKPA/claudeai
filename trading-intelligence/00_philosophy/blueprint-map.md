## The Philosophy of the Relationship

`philosophy_relationship`

*Markets are relationships between participants whose interaction produces price. A trader acts well under uncertainty only by keeping observation apart from inference, estimating probabilities for defined conditions, requiring positive expected value after costs, protecting capital and process, and testing every belief against data.*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — Sections I, IV and XX rest on how orders, liquidity and participants become prices, which is this note's subject.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The philosophy keeps asking that patterns and probabilities be read inside their conditions (III, V, VI, P2, P4), which is the knowledge base's regime filter.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Sections VI and XI (being wrong is part of the system; judge the process, not one outcome) restate the probabilistic mindset this note describes.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The manifesto's 'seek an edge, test it, protect it' matches the note's definition of edge: a repeatable positive expectancy after costs, held by a distribution and not by a single trade.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Sections XVII and XX.4 (keep development apart from evaluation; random patterns can look convincing) are this note's subject.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — Section XIV's recovery asymmetry and survival argument is the drawdown mathematics this note formalises.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Sections XII-XIII and Principle 10 (fear, greed and the urge to recover overriding the process) are this note's subject, including the prospect-theory roots Section XX.3 cites.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Section XVI and Principle 11 make the journal the record of hypotheses and how they were tested, which this note puts into practice.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Section VII's expected-value formula is the KB's expectancy concept with a cost term.

**Supported by**
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Section XX names Harris as a starting point; the book explains who trades, why, and how market structure shapes prices, the base of Sections I and IV.
- **Empirical Market Microstructure** (`mathbook_hasbrouck_empirical_microstructure`) — Also named in Section XX, it supplies the econometrics of trades and quotes (price discovery) needed to test the claim that price expresses interacting orders.
- **All of Statistics** (`mathbook_all_of_statistics`) — It covers the inference toolkit Section XX.2 lists (confidence intervals, bootstrap, hypothesis and multiple testing), which Sections VI, VII and XVII depend on.

**Challenged by**
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — If prices already reflect available information, most conditional tendencies the philosophy wants to measure are noise or quickly arbitraged, so the programme may often end in 'no edge'; the note's adaptive-markets synthesis softens this but does not remove it.

## I. The market is a relationship; price is its visible expression

`philo_market_is_relationship`

*The market is a system of interacting participants with different views of value, different needs and different resources, not a set of numbers. Price is the visible result of their transactions, so understanding price means investigating the conditions, incentives and interactions that produce it.*

**Grounded in**
- **Auction Market Theory** (`concept_auction_market_theory`) — AMT treats the market as a continuous two-sided auction in which price moves to find the other side, the same picture of price emerging from buyers and sellers interacting.
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — The note's account of how orders become prices (limit vs market orders, the spread, resting liquidity being used up) is the mechanism behind 'price is the visible expression of the relationship'.
- **Order Flow** (`concept_order_flow`) — Reading who is aggressive, and whether they succeed, is the knowledge base's way of observing the interaction itself and not only its price outcome.
- **Liquidity** (`concept_liquidity`) — Section I's 'availability of willing counterparties' is liquidity in the microstructure sense: depth and resting orders.
- **Market Sentiment & Positioning** (`concept_market_sentiment`) — Positioning and sentiment gauges (COT, put/call) measure the differing expectations of participants that Section I says meet in the market.
- **Fundamental & Macro Analysis** (`concept_fundamental_macro_analysis`) — Participants' differing views of value come from expectations about rates, growth and liquidity, the incentives and conditions Section I asks the trader to investigate.

**Supported by**
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Harris sorts traders by motive (informed, liquidity providers, utilitarian hedgers and investors, parasitic) and shows how their interaction sets prices and costs, matching Section I's participants with different objectives.
- **Markets and Market Logic** (`book_markets_and_market_logic`) — Steidlmayer's original Market Profile text describes the market as a self-organising auction of price, time and value.
- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Dalton et al. define the market's purpose as facilitating trade and separate day-timeframe traders from the other-timeframe participants who move value: participants with different horizons.
- **Market Microstructure Theory** (`mathbook_ohara_microstructure_theory`) — O'Hara's inventory and information models (Kyle, Glosten-Milgrom) derive price formation from how differently informed participants interact.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros treats prices as the product of participants' biased perceptions interacting with fundamentals, a relationship view of the market.

**Challenged by**
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — On the efficient-market view price already summarises the relationship, so investigating the incentives and interactions behind it should add no exploitable information beyond price itself.
- **Stochastic Processes** (`concept_stochastic_processes`) — Much of quantitative finance (Bachelier, Black-Scholes, Almgren-Chriss) models price as a stochastic process with no reference to participants and still prices and executes well, which suggests some decisions do not need the relationship layer.

**Put into practice by**
- **Andrea Cimitan — Liquidity Auction & Order Flow Strategy** (`strategy_andrea_cimitan_amt`) — It treats the market as a continuous double auction in which urgency, not a count of buyers or sellers, moves price, and its rule 2 locates fair value with the volume profile before any setup.
- **Carmine Rosato — Order Flow Masterclass: Absorption & Stop-Run Reclaim** (`strategy_carmine_rosato_orderflow_masterclass`) — Its core idea is that price moves because one side is more aggressive than the passive liquidity it meets; it reads absorption, liquidity walls and stop runs to watch that interaction live.
- **Jay Awtani — Market DNA: Tape & Level II at DNA Zones** (`strategy_jay_awtani_market_dna`) — Its premise is that only buyers and sellers move price, and rule 4 counts a setup as valid only when aggressive orders overwhelm the passive wall at a DNA zone.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — Its three filters (market state, location, aggression) require reading the auction's balance and the participants' aggression before any trade.
- **The Unified Trading Intelligence Framework** (`synthesis_unified_framework`) — Its L1 stage asks how orders become prices in this instrument (central book, real volume, session liquidity) before context and setups.

**How it could be measured**
- Regress short-horizon price changes on signed order-flow imbalance (Kyle's lambda in formula_sheet) for each instrument, and report how much of the price change the interaction of orders explains.
- Use COT data to show commercials and speculators holding opposite net positions at the same prices, a direct measure of different participants meeting at one price.
- Compare out-of-sample forecasts from price-only features with forecasts that add participant features (volume, delta, positioning).

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no tick, Level 2 or COT data; the evidence lab uses daily bars only.
- No concept note covers heterogeneous beliefs, hedging demand or 'agreeing to disagree', which are the core of the relationship idea.
- Madhavan's microstructure survey, named in Section XX, has no node.

## II. The market as a system governed by rules (the system vs. my model of it)

`philo_market_system_of_rules`

*The market follows real rules that are complex and change over time (supply and demand, information, structure, liquidity, behaviour, economic conditions). No trader can know them fully, so the trader builds a model of the system. The model is not the system and must be tested against the market continually.*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — The institutional rules of trading (order types, matching, the spread) are the most concrete part of 'a system of rules and constraints'.
- **Auction Market Theory** (`concept_auction_market_theory`) — AMT's axioms (price advertises, time regulates, volume measures; balance vs imbalance) are an explicit rule set for how the market works.
- **Liquidity** (`concept_liquidity`) — Section II names liquidity among the forces besides supply and demand that shape prices.
- **Fundamental & Macro Analysis** (`concept_fundamental_macro_analysis`) — Section II's 'economic conditions' (rates, growth, inflation, credit cycles) are covered here.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The claim that factors 'interact differently at different times' is what regimes describe: the same setup can have opposite expectancy in different states.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — The note's adaptive-markets synthesis (efficiency varies with the mix of participants) is the knowledge base's model of how much exploitable structure the system has.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Testing a written rule set on historical data is the knowledge base's main way of holding a model up against the market.

**Supported by**
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo describes market efficiency as an evolving, ecological property: a system with structure whose rules shift as participants adapt.
- **How the Economic Machine Works — Ray Dalio** (`media_dalio_economic_machine`) — Dalio presents the economy as a machine driven by productivity and short- and long-term debt cycles, a rule-governed system that can be modelled.
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper's fallibilism and falsifiability ground the stance that a model is a conjecture to be tested against reality and never confirmed by it.
- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Dalton's auction logic gives the market explicit operating principles (facilitating trade, seeking value) that a trader can model.
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Harris shows how market structures, order types and dealers impose rules that shape the prices we observe.
- **The Econometrics of Financial Markets** (`mathbook_campbell_lo_mackinlay`) — The econometrics of return predictability and asset-pricing tests is the formal practice of testing a model of the market against data.

**Challenged by**
- **Reflexivity** (`concept_reflexivity`) — Soros argues that participants' views change the fundamentals they price, so for widely shared models (such as stops placed at obvious levels) the market is not independent of the understanding used to trade it.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — Structural breaks mean the rules themselves change, not only the trader's knowledge of them, so a model can fail even after describing the system correctly.
- **The Black Swan** (`book_black_swan`) — Taleb argues that rare, high-impact events dominate outcomes and are systematically underestimated, so the part of the system no model captures may matter more than the part it does.

**Put into practice by**
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 2 requires a stated cause for every edge, which helps the trader notice when it stops applying, and rule 11 monitors live results against Monte Carlo ranges, testing the model against the market.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 1 assesses the 'playground' (liquidity, volatility, efficiency, competition) before any setup, and rule 12 makes research continuous because edges decay as more capital trades them.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Its incubation pool and benching rule keep every model under test on new data and retire a model when its drawdown nears the expected maximum.
- **The Unified Trading Intelligence Framework** (`synthesis_unified_framework`) — Its feedback loop from L8 review back to L0 ('is the edge still real?') is the continual testing of the model against the market.

**How it could be measured**
- Count how often live results breach the Monte Carlo envelope of a backtested strategy (the model against the system).
- Run structural-break tests (Chow, CUSUM, Bai-Perron) on strategy returns and on conditional probabilities to measure how often the system's behaviour changes.
- Score the directional hypotheses recorded in the journal (Brier score) over time to measure how well the trader's model tracks the market.
- Run rolling regressions of returns on liquidity, volatility and macro factors to show how their influence changes over time.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No concept note covers model risk or model uncertainty (the gap between the system and the trader's model).
- The evidence lab has no structural-break study.
- Nothing in the knowledge base discusses the tension between Section II's independence claim and reflexivity.

## III. The A → C → B principle: the destination is not the path

`philo_acb_principle`

*A move against the expected direction (A to C) can still fit a larger move to B, so movements must be read at several levels and in context. But B is only a hypothesis, and the trader must tell movement within a structure apart from evidence that the structure itself has changed.*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Structure** (`concept_market_structure`) — Its internal vs external structure, and its rule that a 5-minute CHoCH inside a daily uptrend is usually a pullback, are the vocabulary of A-C-B.
- **Multi-Timeframe Analysis** (`concept_multi_timeframe_analysis`) — Understanding the market 'at different levels' is top-down analysis with separate bias, setup and trigger timeframes.
- **Dow Theory** (`concept_dow_theory`) — Dow's primary trend and secondary reaction, with a trend persisting until a definite reversal, is the century-old form of the principle.
- **Trade Management** (`concept_trade_management`) — Structural stops beyond the level that invalidates the idea are how 'B is a hypothesis' becomes an exit rule.
- **Liquidity Sweep Reversal** (`concept_liquidity_sweep_reversal`) — A move to C that runs stops and then reverses is the knowledge base's best-documented case of an adverse move that belongs to the larger structure.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Treating B as hypothesised rather than predetermined is the probabilistic mindset applied to a target.

**Supported by**
- **The Dow Theory** (`book_dow_theory_rhea`) — Rhea's formal statement of Dow's three trends treats secondary reactions as a normal part of a primary trend.
- **Trading for a Living** (`book_trading_for_a_living`) — Elder's Triple Screen trades with the long-term tide and enters on short-term waves against it, which is the C leg of the principle.
- **Trader Vic: Methods of a Wall Street Master** (`book_trader_vic`) — Sperandeo's 1-2-3 rule gives an objective test for when a trend has changed, the 'structure has changed' half of the principle.
- **Reading Price Charts Bar by Bar** (`book_reading_price_charts_bar_by_bar`) — Brooks's two-legged pullbacks, and his observation that most reversals fail in trends, describe adverse moves that belong to the larger move.
- **The Art and Science of Technical Analysis** (`book_art_science_technical_analysis`) — Grimes studies pullbacks and failure tests as quantified edges, treating the detour as an event that can be measured.
- **Elliott Wave Principle** (`book_elliott_wave_principle`) — The 5-3 wave model makes corrections an expected part of an impulse.

**Challenged by**
- **Elliott Wave Theory** (`concept_elliott_wave`) — The knowledge base itself notes that wave counts are subjective, revisable and hard to falsify, which is the risk that 'A to C is just a detour' can be relabelled forever.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — If price is close to a random walk, an adverse move carries no information beyond geometry; the evidence lab's S1 found P(B | C) at the level of a memoryless null at every depth, so depth alone did not reveal a change of structure.
- **Trend Following** (`concept_trend_following`) — The trend-following tradition does not interpret detours at all and exits on a mechanical trailing rule, which suggests a fixed exit can replace the judgement of 'within the structure' vs 'structure changed'.

**Put into practice by**
- **Trader Mayne — Structure + OTE: HTF Break, POI Pullback & LTF Breaker Entry** (`strategy_trader_mayne_structure_ote`) — After a higher-timeframe market-structure break it expects a pullback into the POI in the discount half of the range, and it enters only on a lower-timeframe breaker with the stop beyond the swept low.
- **Marci Silfrain — Measured Move Trend Strategy: Little RZY** (`strategy_marci_silfrain_measured_move`) — It never enters during the initial impulse, enters on the pullback (Little RZY), and voids the setup on a confirmed close beyond the trendline.
- **Clement Ang — Price Cycle Strategy: Continuation Base and Late-Stage Failed Base** (`strategy_clement_ang_price_cycle`) — It treats a controlled pullback to the 10 and 20 EMAs that becomes a sideways base as continuation, and on shorts a close back above the capping MA is the invalidation.
- **Ted Zhang — Stage Analysis Strategy** (`strategy_ted_zhang_stage_analysis`) — In Stage 2, pullbacks are expected to hold near rising weekly support; a bounce in Stage 4 is not a new uptrend; and losing Stage 2 structure means cut or exit.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Its three-loss rule stops reversion trades after three failures in a row in one session, because the market may be trending and setting a new fair value, which is evidence the structure has changed.

**How it could be measured**
- Evidence-lab S1: P(reach B | touched C_k) against the unconditional rate and a memoryless null, in and out of sample.
- Re-run S1 with swing-based structure (fractal HH/HL, higher vs lower timeframe) in place of a moving-average trend, and on intraday data.
- Test objective structure-change rules (Sperandeo's 1-2-3, a body-close CHoCH) as detectors: compare P(B) after adverse moves of equal depth with and without the rule firing.
- Measure the hindsight gap: P(B) by deepest excursion (known only afterwards) against P(B) by depth touched (known in real time).

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no tested, objective definition of 'the structure has changed'; concept_market_structure only suggests a fractal swing definition.
- S1 uses daily bars and one context variable (a moving-average trend); there is no intraday or multi-timeframe version.
- No rule says which timeframe counts as the 'broader' one.

## IV. Price as the expression of a relationship (observation vs. inference)

`philo_price_expression`

*Price is important evidence but not the whole market. It shows the terms on which participants with different beliefs and needs were willing and able to trade, not why they traded, so observation, interpretation, hypothesis and decision must stay separate steps.*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — Price changes only when aggressive orders use up resting liquidity or quotes are pulled, so a move shows the terms of trade, not motives.
- **Order Flow** (`concept_order_flow`) — Footprint, delta and absorption separate what was traded aggressively from the price result, adding a layer of observation between price and inference.
- **Liquidity** (`concept_liquidity`) — Section IV's 'liquidity available' is part of what price reflects, and the note separates depth from where stops cluster.
- **Auction Market Theory** (`concept_auction_market_theory`) — Acceptance vs rejection (time and volume at a price) is a disciplined way to read what a price move shows.
- **Price Action Trading** (`concept_price_action`) — Its rules (location before pattern, context decides, a trigger is not a signal) limit how far raw price can be interpreted.
- **Volume Profile** (`concept_volume_profile`) — Volume at each price shows where trading was accepted, evidence beyond price itself.
- **Wyckoff Method** (`concept_wyckoff_method`) — Effort vs result (volume against price movement) is an observable test of whether a move reflects pressure or absorption.

**Supported by**
- **Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading** (`paper_kyle_1985`) — Kyle shows that informed traders hide in noise flow and that price impact reveals their information only partly, so a rise cannot be cleanly attributed to 'institutions buying'.
- **Empirical Market Microstructure** (`mathbook_hasbrouck_empirical_microstructure`) — Hasbrouck's price-discovery and information-share methods measure how much of a price change is information and how much is noise.
- **Trades, Quotes and Prices** (`mathbook_bouchaud_trades_quotes_prices`) — Bouchaud et al. treat price moves as the mechanical result of order-book dynamics and metaorder impact, not of a shared view of value.
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Harris shows that traders at the same price can be informed, hedging or supplying liquidity, so one price holds many beliefs.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Kahneman's fast, intuitive System 1 jumps from an observation to a causal story, the error the split between observation and interpretation guards against.
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson separates objective, testable rules from subjective chart interpretation, the same discipline applied to technical analysis.

**Challenged by**
- **Dow Theory** (`concept_dow_theory`) — Dow's first tenet, that the market discounts everything, treats price as a complete summary, the opposite of 'price is not the entire market'.
- **Smart Money Concepts / ICT** (`concept_smart_money_concepts`) — The knowledge base's most-used practitioner lens reads price as the footprint of large participants hunting liquidity. That is an inference from price to intent, which the knowledge base itself calls a narrative, yet many playbooks rest on it.
- **Studies in Tape Reading** (`book_studies_in_tape_reading`) — Wyckoff's tape reading treats inferring large operators' intentions from price and volume as a skill that can be learned. That century-old practice disputes the section's view that such inferences always go beyond what was observed.

**Put into practice by**
- **Andrea Cimitan — Liquidity Auction & Order Flow Strategy** (`strategy_andrea_cimitan_amt`) — Rule 6 tells aggression that moves price (control) apart from aggression that does not (absorption), and rule 8 treats stop runs as liquidity events, not deliberate stop hunting.
- **Carmine Rosato — Order Flow Masterclass: Absorption & Stop-Run Reclaim** (`strategy_carmine_rosato_orderflow_masterclass`) — Rule 3 reads how price responds to delta rather than the delta figure alone, so a strongly positive delta with no rise reads as absorbed buying.
- **Candace Lau — NQ Liquidity Sweep & IFVG Reversal** (`strategy_candace_lau_nq_sweep_reversal`) — Rule 5 says a sweep alone is never an entry because price can sweep and keep going, and rule 7 judges displacement by its speed, not by the finished candle.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 3 requires an entry that leaves no room for interpretation (a 1-minute candle closing above VWAP, not 'price looks strong').
- **Dylan O’Neil — VIX Futures Strategy: VIX Pressure as Confirmation for ES and NQ** (`strategy_dylan_oneil_vix_futures`) — Rule 4 treats an ES break of the prior-day low without VIX confirmation as lacking pressure, so the same price observation is checked against more evidence before continuation is inferred.

**How it could be measured**

**Gaps in the knowledge base**

- No study in the knowledge base measures how often inferences about intent (smart money, the composite operator) are right.

## V. The market as a recurring storyline (recurrence is not repetition)

`philo_recurring_storyline`

*Markets show recurring tendencies, as a long relationship does, but recurrence is not repetition. A past pattern supports only a conditional probability, which applies when present conditions resemble those in which the pattern showed an advantage, and it must be measured and tested.*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The same price formation having different implications under different volatility or trend conditions is the regime idea.
- **Classical Chart Patterns** (`concept_chart_patterns`) — Classical patterns are recurring formations whose statistics (failure rates, average moves) Bulkowski catalogued.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — Whether a pattern recurs is a claim about autocorrelation and stationarity, and this note supplies the tools to test it.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — A recurring tendency is useful only as a repeatable condition with positive expectancy after costs.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Measuring and testing historical tendencies, as the section requires, is backtesting.

**Supported by**
- **Mastering the Market Cycle** (`book_mastering_market_cycle`) — Marks teaches how to recognise where we are in recurring economic, credit and psychological cycles without claiming that they repeat exactly.
- **Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets** (`book_secrets_bull_bear_markets`) — Weinstein's four stages are a recurring storyline whose meaning depends on which stage is active.
- **Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns** (`book_encyclopedia_chart_patterns`) — Bulkowski measures patterns statistically (failure rates, average moves), treating recurrence as a frequency and not a certainty.
- **A Non-Random Walk Down Wall Street** (`book_non_random_walk`) — Lo and MacKinlay reject the random walk with variance-ratio tests, evidence that markets have predictable components.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas holds both that every moment in the market is unique and that an edge is a higher probability of one outcome over another: recurrence without repetition.
- **Trades About to Happen** (`book_trades_about_to_happen`) — Weis's modern Wyckoff reads recurring springs, upthrusts and effort vs result on charts.

**Challenged by**
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson finds that most technical rules fail rigorous testing once data snooping is corrected for, so many apparent storylines may recur no more often than chance.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — The note's working model is to assume most patterns are noise and that edges get crowded and decay, which limits how far any storyline can be trusted.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb shows that people read skill and pattern into luck and survivorship, the bias that makes storylines feel recurrent.

**Put into practice by**
- **Ted Zhang — Stage Analysis Strategy** (`strategy_ted_zhang_stage_analysis`) — Rule 1 classifies the weekly stage with four questions before any trade, and a no-trade list covers stages where the storyline is unclear.
- **Clement Ang — Price Cycle Strategy: Continuation Base and Late-Stage Failed Base** (`strategy_clement_ang_price_cycle`) — It trades a named cycle (uptrend, pullback, wedge pop, cross back, base, breakout) and notes that many of the best bases form during market corrections, reading the storyline in context.
- **Marci Silfrain — Measured Move Trend Strategy: Little RZY** (`strategy_marci_silfrain_measured_move`) — Rule 6 treats the first or second Little RZY of a fresh trend as the strongest and the fourth or fifth as a sign of exhaustion: the same pattern means different things at different points in the story.
- **Andrea Cimitan — Liquidity Auction & Order Flow Strategy** (`strategy_andrea_cimitan_amt`) — Rule 1 uses order-flow autocorrelation (strong pressure in one session raises the odds of similar behaviour in the next) as a probable recurrence, not a certain one.
- **Tanja Trades — AMD Futures Model: Trade the Distribution Leg** (`strategy_tanja_amd_futures`) — It reads the AMD sequence only after rule 1 has classified the day as high or low probability from the economic calendar.

**How it could be measured**
- Evidence-lab S2: conditional cells (regime x volatility x pattern) found in sample, then classed out of sample as held, weakened, vanished or reversed.
- Analogue test: with a similarity metric on vectors of conditions, check whether the spread of outcomes narrows as similarity to past cases rises.
- Track a pattern's hit rate decade by decade to measure decay.
- Pattern statistics by regime (failure rate, average move) in the style of Bulkowski.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no definition or metric for 'sufficiently similar' conditions.
- Only one simple pattern (three lower closes) has been tested; the storyline frameworks (Wyckoff, AMD, stage analysis) have not.
- There is no data on how edges decay after they are published or crowded.

## VI. Probability as the foundation of decisions; the law of large numbers; probability is conditional

`philo_conditional_probability`

*The future is uncertain, so decisions rest on probability. A probability holds only for a defined event, horizon and set of conditions. Many relevant observations are needed (the law of large numbers), and even a large sample does not guarantee the estimate will persist if conditions change.*

**Grounded in**
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The note's thinking-in-bets mindset, and its warning that a win rate after 30 trades has a wide confidence interval, match Section VI.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — The knowledge base's note on distributions treats a win rate as a posterior belief updated with evidence, not a constant.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — An edge is a property of the distribution of outcomes under a repeatable condition, which is the quantity Section VI asks the trader to estimate.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The section's example of a pattern in trending vs ranging markets is the regime filter.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Measuring how often outcomes occurred under defined conditions is done by backtesting with realistic costs and out-of-sample tests.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — The warning that a few successes or a lucky sample do not establish an advantage is this note's subject.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — The journal's regime label and per-setup statistics turn live trades into conditional observations.

**Supported by**
- **Introduction to Probability** (`mathbook_blitzstein_intro_probability`) — An intuition-first text built on conditioning and expectation, the formal basis of 'probability depends on the conditions'.
- **An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications** (`mathbook_feller_probability`) — Feller's classic treatment of limit theorems and random walks states the law of large numbers and its assumptions.
- **Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** (`mathbook_jaynes_probability_logic`) — Jaynes treats every probability as conditional on stated information, which is the section's principle in its strongest form.
- **All of Statistics** (`mathbook_all_of_statistics`) — Confidence intervals, the bootstrap and hypothesis testing answer the section's question of how reliable an estimate is.
- **Against the Gods** (`book_against_the_gods`) — Bernstein's history of probability and risk traces the ideas of sampling and the law of large numbers that the section relies on.
- **Harvard Stat 110: Probability — Joe Blitzstein** (`media_harvard_stat110`) — Blitzstein's course covers conditional probability, expectation and limit theorems.
- **3Blue1Brown — Bayes theorem, the geometry of changing beliefs** (`media_3b1b_bayes`) — It explains Bayes' theorem as updating a belief on new evidence, the mechanism for conditioning an estimate.

**Challenged by**
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Under fat tails the law of large numbers converges far more slowly and sample statistics can mislead, so more observations may not bring an estimate near the truth within a trader's time horizon.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — With structural breaks and non-stationarity, adding older data can mix regimes, so a bigger sample can lower relevance even as it lowers variance; the section names this trade-off but does not resolve it.
- **The Black Swan** (`book_black_swan`) — Rare, high-impact events may be missing from any historical sample, so a frequency estimate can be most wrong exactly where the losses are largest.

**Put into practice by**
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — The standard error SE(p) = sqrt(p(1-p)/n), which is about plus or minus 7% at n = 50, and the SQN t-statistic quantify how reliable an estimated win rate is.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Item 1 requires at least 30 logged instances with positive expectancy after costs, and item 2 requires the current regime to match the setup's valid regime.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — It judges rules by whether they have a positive expected return over a large sample, and uses Monte Carlo resampling to estimate the chance of a given loss or losing streak.
- **Steven Dux — Small-Cap Short Statistics Playbook** (`strategy_steven_dux_small_cap_short`) — Its statistics are defined on a stated population (market cap, float under 50M, price above $3 because cheaper names distort the statistics) and reported per float band.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 7 requires in- and out-of-sample periods that include different regimes, plus cross-market tests, and rule 6 requires at least two trades a month so a sample builds up.
- **Tori Trades — Trendline Trading Playbook: Action Line and Safety Line** (`strategy_tori_trendline_4h`) — Rule 8 logs 2-touch and 3-touch trendline breaks separately, so each condition gets its own probability estimate.

**How it could be measured**
- Calibration of the probabilities journaled before each trade (Brier score, reliability curve).
- Confidence-interval width against sample size for each setup; evidence-lab S3 found that a true +0.05R edge looks like a loser in about a third of 30- and 100-trade samples.
- Stability of conditional estimates across rolling windows and between in- and out-of-sample periods (evidence-lab S2).
- The section's own horizon example: P(up) over one hour against one day under the same condition.
- A runs test or autocorrelation of trade outcomes, to check the independence the law of large numbers assumes.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no calibration or Brier-score tool.
- formula_sheet has no Bayesian (beta-binomial) update formula, although the concept notes recommend Bayesian updating.
- Nothing covers the law of large numbers when outcomes are dependent or non-stationary.
- There is no intraday data to test the horizon dependence the section describes.

## VII. Mathematics, the trader's sword (expected value; maths must serve the decision)

`philo_mathematics_sword`

*Mathematics turns observations into testable claims: how often, how large the wins and losses, how variable, what costs, and whether the result holds out of sample. A strategy is judged by expected value after costs and by the whole distribution of outcomes, not by its win rate. The simplest adequate model is preferred because the mathematics must serve the decision.*

**Grounded in**
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The section's EV formula with costs, and its point that win rate is not expectancy, are this note's content.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The note defines edge as E = p*W - (1-p)*L - costs > 0, the same expression the owner uses.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — The breakeven win rate 1/(1+RR) shows why a strategy can win often and still lose money, and the reverse.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — Profit factor, SQN, skew and kurtosis describe the 'complete distribution of outcomes'.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — The section's demand for tests on data not used in development is met by this note's defences (holdout, walk-forward, deflated Sharpe).
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Realistic costs, no look-ahead and out-of-sample tests are how the section's questions get answered.
- **Optimal Execution & Market Impact** (`concept_optimal_execution_market_impact`) — Whether a pattern stays useful after costs depends on spread, slippage and market impact, which this note models.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Examining the whole distribution and not only averages matters because returns have fat tails and skew.

**Supported by**
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp expresses results as expectancy in R-multiples and argues that expectancy, together with sizing, drives results more than entries do.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's asymmetry between how often and how much a strategy wins makes the same point as 'a trader can win frequently and still lose money'.
- **Beat the Dealer** (`book_beat_the_dealer`) — Thorp proved that a measurable edge exists in blackjack and sized his bets to it, the model of mathematics deciding whether an advantage is real.
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson applies statistical inference and data-snooping corrections to show which trading rules survive: mathematics used to test beliefs.
- **Advances in Financial Machine Learning** (`book_advances_financial_ml`) — López de Prado gives backtest-overfitting statistics and purged cross-validation for keeping development apart from evaluation.
- **The Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (`paper_deflated_sharpe_2014`) — The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects performance for the number of trials, skew and kurtosis, a direct answer to whether results held beyond the development data.
- **Inside the Black Box** (`book_inside_the_black_box`) — Narang's anatomy of a quant strategy puts a transaction-cost model beside the alpha model, matching the section's costs term.
- **The Man Who Solved the Market** (`book_man_who_solved_the_market`) — Renaissance is described as exploiting small statistical edges at scale with scientists and data, mathematics as the main instrument.

**Challenged by**
- **Kelly Criterion & Optimal f** (`concept_kelly_criterion_optimal_f`) — Kelly maximises expected log growth, not expected value: betting more than twice the Kelly fraction gives negative growth even with an edge, so positive EV per trade is necessary but not enough.
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Under fat tails the sample mean and Sharpe ratio can be dominated by a few observations, so the 'complete distribution' a trader estimates may not show the tail that decides the outcome.
- **Antifragile** (`book_antifragile`) — Taleb argues for shaping convex payoffs rather than relying on probability estimates, an alternative to evaluating strategies by expected value.

**Put into practice by**
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It lists E[R] = p*b - q, the breakeven win rate, SQN, the per-trade cost formula and the deflated Sharpe ratio, the tools that answer the section's questions.
- **Lance Breitstein — Capitulation Mean Reversion** (`strategy_lance_breitstein_mean_reversion`) — It judges every trade by EV = (win rate x reward) - (loss probability x risk), and requires a good setup to raise the odds, offer a large reward and have defined risk.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 5 reviews net profit, win rate, average trade, average winner and loser, maximum drawdown and losing streaks, and rule 6 freezes the rules after an 80/20 in-sample/out-of-sample split.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — It keeps to two or three entry rules and one exit, rejects strategies that work only at one parameter value, and ranks Monte Carlo drawdown above backtest drawdown.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 2 checks the expected move per trade against costs; in the page's example about 0.4% per trade meets about 0.2% round-trip cost, so costs take half the edge.
- **Consensus & Conflicts across sources** (`synthesis_consensus_conflicts`) — It settles high R:R vs high win rate with 'only expectancy after costs matters', and uses expectancy to show that a 90% win rate at 1:20 is implausible.

**How it could be measured**
- EV per trade after costs, with a bootstrap confidence interval, from backtests and from the journal.
- The full distribution: skew, kurtosis, MAE/MFE, maximum drawdown, longest losing streak.
- The ratio of out-of-sample to in-sample performance, and the deflated Sharpe ratio given the number of variants tried (evidence-lab S5).
- Break-even cost: the cost per trade at which EV falls to zero (evidence-lab S7).
- Expected log growth at the planned risk fraction, reported next to arithmetic EV.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no cost data per instrument (spreads, commissions, slippage).
- Nothing records how many variants were tested, which the deflated Sharpe ratio needs.
- The section's $7 example gives only averages; the knowledge base has no worked example of the same strategy's outcome variance or log growth.

## VIII. The source of a trading opportunity: understanding + evidence + mathematics

`philo_source_of_opportunity`

*An opportunity exists only when three things agree: a defensible, testable explanation of what the setup represents (understanding), present conditions that match those in which it showed an advantage (evidence), and a favourable assessment of outcomes against risk after costs and estimate uncertainty (mathematics). When they disagree, the disagreement is investigated, not resolved in favour of the trade the trader wants.*

**Grounded in**
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The KB defines edge as a cause (someone on the other side for a structural reason), a detector, an execution path and a survival layer, which maps almost one-to-one onto understanding, evidence and mathematics.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The mathematics leg is expected value per trade in R, and the note's table shows a high win rate can hide negative expectancy, as Section VIII says a high probability of winning is not sufficient.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The evidence leg asks whether the present market fits the conditions where the setup worked, and the regimes note states the same setup has opposite expectancy in different regimes.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Section VIII's worry that the apparent pattern may not be statistically meaningful is the overfitting problem, and the note's first defence (hypothesis first: an economic or microstructural reason) is the understanding leg.
- **Trade Execution** (`concept_trade_execution`) — Transaction costs sit inside Section VIII's mathematics leg, and the execution note lists spread, commission, slippage and missed fills as the costs that silently consume small edges.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — The breakeven win rate 1/(1+RR) is the simplest test of whether a probability estimate is favourable relative to payoff and risk.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The 'uncertainty surrounding the estimates' corresponds to the note's point that a win-rate estimate after 30 trades has a wide confidence interval and is a belief to be updated.

**Supported by**
- **Inside the Black Box** (`book_inside_the_black_box`) — Narang decomposes a strategy into alpha, risk, transaction-cost and portfolio-construction models, an independent statement that a signal is not an opportunity until costs and risk agree with it.
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson applies the scientific method and data-snooping corrections to technical rules and finds most fail, supporting the demand that an explanation be backed by evidence.
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp argues that expectancy and position sizing, not entries, drive results, which is the mathematics leg.
- **Beat the Dealer** (`book_beat_the_dealer`) — Thorp's card counting combines all three legs: a causal reason (deck composition), an observable present condition (the count) and a measured advantage that sets the bet size.
- **Market Wizards** (`book_market_wizards`) — The KB's synthesis records Kovner and Marcus using fundamentals for why and technicals for when, an explanation plus current evidence before acting.
- **Finding Alphas** (`mathbook_finding_alphas`) — WorldQuant's guide treats an alpha as an idea that must be designed and then tested before use, the same idea-to-evidence pipeline.

**Challenged by**
- **The Man Who Solved the Market** (`book_man_who_solved_the_market`) — The KB's account of Renaissance credits data and many small statistical edges at scale, suggesting evidence and mathematics can carry a decision without a human-readable explanation, so the understanding leg may be less necessary than Section VIII implies.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — If prices are close to efficient, most apparent agreements between story, evidence and maths are noise, and the adaptive-markets view adds that real edges decay, so the evidence leg can be stale by the time it is measured.

**Put into practice by**
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 2 requires explaining why the edge should exist, rule 3 defines objective entry, exit and size, and rules 5-8 backtest, freeze the rules, test out of sample and run Monte Carlo: understanding, evidence and mathematics in sequence.
- **Lance Breitstein — Capitulation Mean Reversion** (`strategy_lance_breitstein_mean_reversion`) — It scores each extreme move on a checklist (size, speed, no fundamental change, forced flows, value anchor) and judges every trade on EV = win rate x reward - loss probability x risk.
- **Traveling Trader — Universal Trading Strategy** (`strategy_traveling_trader_universal`) — A trade is valid only when the whole story is present (liquidity catalyst, displacement, retracement), and rule 4 requires confluence and forbids trading a single signal.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Line 1 requires 30 or more logged instances with positive expectancy after costs and line 2 requires the current regime to match the setup's valid regime, scoring the evidence and mathematics legs before entry.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — E[R] = p*b - q, the breakeven win rate and the standard error of an estimated win rate give the mathematics leg concrete tests.
- **The Unified Trading Intelligence Framework** (`synthesis_unified_framework`) — Its L0 stage asks why the pattern should pay and who is on the other side before any setup is considered, the understanding leg stated as a gate.

**How it could be measured**
- Tag each journaled trade with three flags (written explanation, conditions matched, EV after costs > 0) and compare realised expectancy and its bootstrap confidence interval for trades where all three agree versus two of three.
- Log every case where the explanation and the quantitative evidence disagreed, including trades not taken, and record what price did afterwards, to measure the value of 'investigate the contradiction, do not force'.
- Require the Wilson lower bound of each setup's win rate to exceed its cost-adjusted breakeven win rate 1/(1+b) before it counts as an opportunity, and track how many setups pass.
- Compare out-of-sample decay (OOS/IS expectancy ratio) of setups with a written causal reason against purely data-mined variants to test whether the understanding leg improves persistence.
- Recompute EV with costs at 1x and 2x realistic levels to see which setups survive.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No rubric defines what makes an explanation 'defensible' or how to score the understanding leg.
- Most of the 47 playbook notes state that no expectancy check is possible because win rate or R:R is missing, so the mathematics leg cannot be computed for them.
- No per-instrument cost table (spread, commission, typical slippage) exists to feed the cost term.
- No data on how often the three legs disagree in practice or what happens in those cases.

## IX. When understanding fails, step back (not trading is a decision)

`philo_step_back`

*When the trader cannot explain what the market is doing, the right response is to wait: continuous watching breeds over-interpretation and a compulsion to act, so standing aside until a recognisable, testable situation appears protects capital and attention, and not trading is itself a deliberate decision.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Overconfidence, recency and confirmation bias feeding over-trading, plus FOMO, are the mechanisms Section IX says constant watching produces.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The regime grid's choppy high-volatility quadrant prescribes reducing size or standing aside, a regime-level form of stepping back.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — A plan's regime filter (when the playbook is on or off) and psychology rules (maximum trades, a break after N losses) turn stepping back into a predefined rule.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — The note's working model, that most patterns are noise and evidence must be demanded, explains why closely watched movements may have no predictive value.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Inventing an explanation for each fluctuation is the discretionary form of testing many variants: with enough looks something appears meaningful by chance.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — An edge is a repeatable condition with positive expectancy; outside that condition a trade has no measured expectancy, so there is nothing to act on.

**Supported by**
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb argues that looking at P&L too frequently mostly shows noise, the same mechanism as Section IX's warning that constant observation encourages over-interpretation.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Kahneman's account of fast heuristic judgement and overconfidence explains why traders see opportunities that are not there.
- **The Mental Game of Trading** (`book_mental_game_of_trading`) — Tendler diagnoses greed, fear and tilt as the root flaws that push traders into trades their method does not justify.
- **The Hour Between Dog and Wolf** (`book_hour_between_dog_wolf`) — Coates links hormonal cycles to risk-taking, supporting the claim that emotional involvement in a moving market distorts the decision to act.

**Challenged by**
- **Way of the Turtle** (`book_way_of_the_turtle`) — Faith reports that most Turtles who failed did so by not taking every signal and destroying the distribution, so discretionary stepping back can remove the rare large winners an edge depends on.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The note says to execute every valid setup because skipping trades destroys the sample the edge depends on, so stepping back is safe only if 'unclear' is defined in advance rather than felt in the moment.
- **The Whipsaw Song — Ed Seykota** (`media_seykota_whipsaw_song`) — Trend followers accept repeated whipsaws as the price of catching big trends, which often begin in exactly the ambiguous conditions a trader would step back from.

**Put into practice by**
- **Anthony Crudele — Futures Trend Trading Strategy: Bollinger Band Environments** (`strategy_anthony_crudele_futures_trend`) — After the 50% mean-reversion target is reached, rule 5 keeps 'hands in pocket' until the environment is clear again, and the no-trade rule says to stand aside whenever the environment is unclear.
- **Tanja Trades — AMD Futures Model: Trade the Distribution Leg** (`strategy_tanja_amd_futures`) — Rule 3 says no clear draw on liquidity means no trade, rule 1 classifies low-probability days (bank holidays, the day before CPI, FOMC or NFP), and rule 10 says to stand aside without a clear bias or structure.
- **Forrest Knight — Volume Profile Edge-to-Edge Strategy** (`strategy_forrest_knight_volume_profile`) — Its built-in filter is to sit out when no valid edge signal appears, and rule 9 forbids trades in the low-volume middle of the profile.
- **Fredy Sarmiento — Institutional Options Flow: Gamma Wall Reversal** (`strategy_fredy_sarmiento_options_flow`) — Rule 3 allows no trade while price sits between meaningful levels, even for a whole day, and rule 10 says not to trade just because the market is open.
- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rule 9 has the trader weigh the emotional expected value of another trade when out of the zone and accept that the best trade may be none, and rule 8 ends the session if the reset limit is broken.
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 1 drops a chart from the watchlist when the hourly trend is hard to read, and rule 4 forbids inventing new plans on the spot after the open.

**How it could be measured**
- Record 'stood aside' as an explicit journal decision with its reason, then track what the skipped situation did (a shadow journal) to estimate losses avoided versus opportunities missed.
- Compare the expectancy of pre-planned trades with trades decided on the spot without a written plan.
- Plot average R against a trade's sequence number within the session to test whether later, marginal trades have lower expectancy.
- Rate clarity (1-5) before each potential trade and test whether clarity ratings predict outcomes.
- Compare results on regime-tagged choppy or unclear days with clear-trend days to check whether standing aside on the former improves the equity curve.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No objective definition of 'unclear': the regimes note gives detectors (ADX, Hurst, AMT balance) but no stand-aside thresholds.
- The journal concept note has no field for no-trade decisions, so the cost of waiting cannot be measured.
- No concept note on attention, screen time or overtrading; Barber and Odean's overtrading evidence is referenced in blueprint.yaml but has no node in the allowed index.
- No KB data on how often large moves begin from conditions that looked unclear beforehand.

## X. When the evidence changes (manage risk first, then investigate)

`philo_evidence_changes`

*When the evidence behind a position changes, the trader first applies the predefined risk and invalidation rules, and only then investigates whether the change is normal variation, a new factor, a new market environment or a flawed original interpretation, recording the findings and admitting when no explanation can be found.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trade Management** (`concept_trade_management`) — Structural stops beyond the level that invalidates the idea, and time stops, are the predefined rules Section X says to act on before investigating.
- **Market Structure** (`concept_market_structure`) — BOS versus CHoCH, and the rule that a 5-minute CHoCH inside a daily uptrend is usually a pullback, are tools for separating movement within a structure from a change of structure.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Section X's question about a different environment is the regime question, and the KB names running a setup in the wrong regime as the most common reason a strategy appears to stop working.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — Measuring the size and speed of a change against normal variation needs a volatility yardstick such as ATR multiples, which the note uses for stops and expected range.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — The note's warning that relationships change, with tests for structural breaks and rolling estimates, is the statistical form of asking whether the market changed.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The note names Bayesian updating of the edge estimate as the formal version of letting the market prove the trader wrong.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Section X records the investigation in the journal, and the review cadence includes an edge-decay check against backtest expectations.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Invalidation must be written before entry for the 'manage risk first' step to be predefined rather than improvised.

**Supported by**
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's principles of predefining risk and fully accepting it beforehand mean the exit is not renegotiated when the evidence turns.
- **Best Loser Wins** (`book_best_loser_wins`) — Hougaard's core rule is to take small losses instantly and without drama, which is Section X's first responsibility.
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** (`book_reminiscences_stock_operator`) — Livermore's lessons to cut losses, never average down and treat hope as poison argue against staying exposed because one wants the original prediction to succeed.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros's real-time diary records hypotheses, positions and changes of mind, and his method is to look for what is wrong with the thesis, matching investigate-then-record.
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper's falsifiability requires stating in advance what would refute a hypothesis, the logic behind predefined invalidation.
- **Time Series Analysis** (`mathbook_hamilton_time_series`) — Hamilton's regime-switching models give a formal way to ask whether recent observations come from a new environment rather than ordinary variation.
- **Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** (`mathbook_jaynes_probability_logic`) — Jaynes treats probability as extended logic updated by evidence, the formal frame for revising a hypothesis without abandoning disciplined reasoning.

**Challenged by**
- **Liquidity Sweep Reversal** (`concept_liquidity_sweep_reversal`) — Stops cluster beyond obvious swing levels, so price can sweep through a predefined invalidation level and then reverse, meaning a 'manage risk first' exit can fire precisely on an A->C->B detour.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Gaps and fast markets mean a stop does not guarantee the planned loss, so the predefined rule may not be executable at the intended price.
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Under fat tails sample statistics converge slowly, so asking whether a move was consistent with normal variation may not be answerable from the sample a trader has.
- **Brando Elite — Options Swing Trading on HTF Levels + News Catalysts** (`strategy_brando_options_swing`) — It sizes the whole option position so it can go to zero instead of using a tight stop, explicitly to avoid premature exits during volatility, an alternative to exiting on changed evidence.

**Put into practice by**
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 12 does not pull a strategy for short-term underperformance but cuts size or moves it to simulation when drawdown nears its Monte Carlo maximum or underperformance persists, keeping benched strategies for possible return.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 11 monitors live results against Monte Carlo ranges and treats a breach as a warning to review, not automatic proof the strategy is broken.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — The Three-Loss Rule stops reversion trades after three failed reversions in a session because the market may be trending to a new fair value, and rule 9 drops an old fair price once value has clearly moved.
- **Fredy Sarmiento — Institutional Options Flow: Gamma Wall Reversal** (`strategy_fredy_sarmiento_options_flow`) — Rule 9 says that if price breaks an options wall with strength and holds beyond it, the trader stops fading the level and reassesses which level now dominates.
- **Ted Zhang — Stage Analysis Strategy** (`strategy_ted_zhang_stage_analysis`) — Rule 5 sets invalidation before entry and exits when Stage 2 structure is lost, and the Moderna example shows exiting a failed breakout as the correct response.

**How it could be measured**
- After every invalidation exit, record whether price later reached the original target within a fixed horizon, to estimate how often exits fall on detours versus genuine failures, and compare structural with volatility-based stop placement on this measure.
- Express the adverse move at exit in ATR units and compare it with the MAE distribution of past winning trades of the same setup.
- Classify each changed-evidence event (normal variation, new factor, regime change, interpretation error, unexplained) in the journal and track frequencies; many confident explanations with poor later accuracy signals story-making.
- Compare live drawdowns and losing streaks with Monte Carlo bands from the backtest and count breaches.
- Run change-point or rolling-expectancy tests on each setup's R-multiple series.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No setup in the KB has a documented 'normal variation' band, such as the MAE distribution of its winners.
- Structural-break and change-point detection appears only as a bullet in the time-series note, not as a worked procedure for trade results.
- No data on how often price sweeps through structural stops and then reaches the original target.
- What to do after a review (pause, cut size, retire) is left unspecified even in the most systematic playbooks.

## XI. Being wrong is part of the system (decision quality vs. outcome)

`philo_being_wrong`

*Losses are built into probabilistic decisions: one result does not show whether a decision was good, because quality rests on the reasoning, evidence and risk at the time. Losing runs must still be tested against the strategy's expected variation and limits, so the trader accepts being wrong about outcomes while refusing to be careless about reasoning.*

**Grounded in**
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Douglas's truth that any single outcome is possible and Duke's warning against judging decisions by results are the KB's statement that one trade's outcome is not feedback on the decision.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — A positive-expectancy system still produces losses; the note treats edge as a property of the distribution, not of any one trade.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — The note shows long losing streaks are likely even for good systems (10 or more in 500 trades at a 40% win rate), the variation Section XI says a sound strategy can produce.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — SQN, essentially a t-statistic of mean R, gives a way to tell whether results are consistent with an edge or with zero.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Rule-adherence and emotion fields separate execution quality from P&L, and the review compares live results with backtest expectations.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Monte Carlo resampling of trade order gives the expected distribution of drawdowns and streaks against which a live losing run is judged.

**Supported by**
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's central thesis is to separate decision quality from outcome and avoid 'resulting', the distinction Section XI draws.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas holds that wins and losses are randomly distributed for any edge, so a single loss carries little information about the decision.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's alternative histories judge a decision by the distribution of outcomes it could have produced, not the one that occurred.
- **Best Loser Wins** (`book_best_loser_wins`) — Hougaard argues the winning trader is the one who accepts being wrong many times and loses well.
- **An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications** (`mathbook_feller_probability`) — Feller's treatment of random walks and ruin problems gives the mathematics of runs that makes consecutive losses expected rather than surprising.
- **MIT 15.S50 Poker Theory and Analytics** (`media_mit_15s50_poker`) — MIT's poker analytics course studies decisions under uncertainty through expectancy, a setting where sound decisions routinely lose individual hands.

**Challenged by**
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Taleb shows sample statistics converge slowly under fat tails, so the 'adequate sample' needed to tell variance from a broken strategy may be far larger than traders accumulate.
- **Ed Seykota** (`person_ed_seykota`) — Seykota's view that everybody gets what they want out of the market treats a pattern of outcomes as information about the trader's hidden intentions, not just variance.
- **Prop-Firm Evaluations & Risk Rules** (`concept_prop_firm_risk_rules`) — Trailing drawdown limits make results path-dependent, so a run of sound but losing decisions can end the account and outcomes carry consequences that decision quality does not protect against.

**Put into practice by**
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 17 journals every trade with mistakes, emotional state and a trade grade, and says to grade the process, not the P&L.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Stage 4 tracks consistency week over week by judging execution rather than outcome, and its principles warn that one green month does not mean the method is solved.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Monte Carlo drawdown, not backtest drawdown, sets expectations, and rule 12 says short-term underperformance alone is not a reason to pull a strategy.
- **Marci Silfrain — Measured Move Trend Strategy: Little RZY** (`strategy_marci_silfrain_measured_move`) — Its risk section (carried from earlier T3 notes) tells the trader to plan to lose on every trade and to expect the next 20 trades to lose in a row.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Rule 1 logs mistakes without judgment as information, separating process errors from the P&L they produced.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — The standard error of a win rate, the SQN t-statistic and the streak-loss formula 1 - (1-f)^k quantify whether a losing run is within expected variation.

**How it could be measured**
- Build a 2x2 matrix from the journal (process followed or not x win or loss) and compare expectancy by cell.
- Compute the probability of the observed losing streak given the estimated win rate and number of trades, and flag runs outside a chosen band such as the 95th percentile.
- Place the current live drawdown on the backtest's Monte Carlo drawdown distribution.
- Report a Wilson interval on the win rate and the SQN of R-multiples, plus the number of trades needed for the interval to exclude breakeven.
- Correlate rule-adherence rate with R per trade over rolling windows.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No rubric scores decision quality independently of P&L; the playbooks that grade trades leave their grading criteria unspecified.
- No pre-agreed thresholds for when a losing run counts as evidence that assumptions have failed; the Monte Carlo percentile is left open.
- Sample-size guidance for fat-tailed, skewed R distributions is missing from the concept notes.

## XII. Protect the process, not the ego

`philo_protect_process`

*Money creates pressure that pushes traders to abandon a tested method after losses or stretch it after wins. The trader follows the tested process rather than the desired outcome, and revises the method only through disciplined investigation of evidence, never under emotional pressure: consistent principles, flexible hypotheses, disciplined execution.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Loss aversion, the disposition effect, overconfidence and revenge trading in the note are the fear, greed, frustration and excitement Section XII lists.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Rules written before the trade, including maximum trades and breaks after losses, are the process being protected.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — The rule to size down in drawdowns and size up only with evidence addresses the unjustified risk increase after wins that Section XII warns against.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Accepting that single outcomes carry little information is what lets a trader keep executing after a loss instead of abandoning the method.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Rule-adherence and emotion-tag fields expose psychology leaks, the data needed to tell disciplined revision from emotional drift.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Changing rules after a few losses fits the method to a tiny recent sample, while out-of-sample and walk-forward testing is the evidence-based route to revision.

**Supported by**
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's seven principles of consistency end with never violating them, and he places the source of inconsistency in beliefs about risk.
- **The Disciplined Trader** (`book_disciplined_trader`) — Douglas argues mental frameworks, especially beliefs about risk and being wrong, are the bottleneck to following a method.
- **The Mental Game of Trading** (`book_mental_game_of_trading`) — Tendler maps greed, fear, tilt and confidence problems to underlying flaws, matching Section XII's catalogue of emotional interference.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Prospect theory and loss aversion, which Section XX names as a research base, explain why traders abandon methods after losses.
- **The Psychology of Trading** (`book_psychology_of_trading_steenbarger`) — Steenbarger changes trading patterns through structured self-observation, a disciplined alternative to emotional rule changes.
- **The Hour Between Dog and Wolf** (`book_hour_between_dog_wolf`) — Coates ties hormonal shifts to changing risk appetite, a biological basis for overconfidence after wins.
- **Way of the Turtle** (`book_way_of_the_turtle`) — Faith attributes most Turtle failures to psychology rather than the rules: traders given the same tested method diverged by how faithfully they executed it.

**Challenged by**
- **Stanley Druckenmiller — Lost Tree Club speech** (`media_druckenmiller_lost_tree_2015`) — Druckenmiller describes heavy concentration and Soros doubling the 1992 sterling short to 200%, conviction-driven sizing outside any uniform rule behind some of the largest results in the KB.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — It holds that psychology is overrated early and that being overwhelmed by emotion means the size is too large, implying the fix is structural (smaller size, clearer rules) rather than resisting emotion.
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo's view that edges appear and decay means a tested process can stop working for reasons outside the trader, so the line between 'a few losses' and real decay is harder to draw than Section XII suggests.

**Put into practice by**
- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rule 8's mid-session reset (name the emotion, move, refocus, set a limit) ends the session if the limit is broken, and rule 10 closes each day with an If-Then plan.
- **Dhesi — Liquidity Inversion Model: Sweep, FVG Inversion & LTF Confirmation** (`strategy_dhesi_liquidity_inversion`) — Rule 14 ends the day after two losses, and the risk section forbids moving the structural stop because the dollar amount feels uncomfortable.
- **Clement Ang — Price Cycle Strategy: Continuation Base and Late-Stage Failed Base** (`strategy_clement_ang_price_cycle`) — Its tilt control caps re-entries at about three attempts per ticker, after which the trader stops trading the name.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Rule 8 grades a trade before sizing it (a C trade with A+ risk is 'hope with leverage'), and the no-trade rules forbid dropping the process because of friction.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 9 runs deployed strategies with no discretionary overrides, and they change only through the rule 12 bench-and-replace process tied to Monte Carlo drawdown.
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 13 hides the running P&L and manages from the chart, and rule 15 allows one re-entry after a stop and never a third.

**How it could be measured**
- Compare the rule-adherence rate of trades taken after a loss with those taken after a win.
- Test whether position size, as % of equity at risk, changes after winning or losing streaks; under a fixed-fractional rule it should not.
- Compare the expectancy of trades tagged fear, FOMO or revenge with neutral-state trades.
- Keep a rule-change log and measure what share of changes followed a documented test on N or more trades versus a short losing run.
- Measure the R of trades taken after a daily loss limit would have ended the session.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB note covers versioning a method: when a rule change is justified, how it is tested and how the old version is retired.
- Links between emotion tags and outcomes rest on T4 practitioner heuristics with no data in the KB.
- Prospect theory and the disposition effect are cited only through summaries; the underlying papers have no node in the allowed index.

## XIII. Follow the right decisions, not the money

`philo_decisions_not_money`

*Starting from 'how much can I make?' distorts judgment and makes every move look like an opportunity. The trader first asks about understanding, evidence, model fit, probabilities, the cost of being wrong, reward against risk and consistency with the process, and only then considers the money, accepting missed moves without abandoning discipline.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — FOMO and greed, listed in the note, are how the pursuit of money makes every movement look like an opportunity.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Planning the trade and trading the plan puts the established process ahead of the trade's payoff.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Money is evaluated through expected value per trade after the decision questions are answered, not as a target in itself.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — Expressing outcomes in R rather than dollars turns 'is the potential return sufficient to justify the risk?' into a ratio instead of a wish.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Process over outcome, as the note frames Duke and Douglas, is the probabilistic basis for following decisions rather than P&L.

**Supported by**
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp moves attention from dollars to R-multiples and expectancy, so performance is judged by the quality of the system rather than the money made.
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's process-over-outcome stance supports answering the decision questions before looking at the payoff.
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** (`book_reminiscences_stock_operator`) — Livermore's lesson that tips and hope are poison records repeated losses from trading on desire rather than on conditions.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's consistency principles (identify edges objectively, predefine and accept risk) put the process ahead of the money.

**Challenged by**
- **The Zurich Axioms** (`book_zurich_axioms`) — Gunther's axiom to always bet meaningfully argues that a speculator who keeps money out of the decision will not stake enough for good decisions to matter.

**Put into practice by**
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Its foundational principles state that the goal is a repeatable process, not a dollar figure, with money as a by-product.
- **Dhesi — Liquidity Inversion Model: Sweep, FVG Inversion & LTF Confirmation** (`strategy_dhesi_liquidity_inversion`) — Rule 11 sets targets at liquidity pools and never from dollar goals or arbitrary point counts.
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 13 hides the running P&L during the trade and rule 17 grades the process rather than the P&L.
- **Brando Elite — Options Swing Trading on HTF Levels + News Catalysts** (`strategy_brando_options_swing`) — Rule 1 puts mindset first: emotional stability, accepting that not every day is a trading day, and learning ahead of quick money.
- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rule 5 asks for motivation from goals, growth and mastery rather than money alone, and sets one process goal per session.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Its ten gates (edge, regime, session, bias, location, trigger, invalidation, R:R, size, state of mind) contain no money target, and size is derived from equity x risk% / stop.

**How it could be measured**
- Add a journal field recording whether a trade was prompted by the plan or by a P&L goal (a daily target, recovering a loss), and compare expectancy.
- Measure how trade frequency and size change as the account approaches a daily profit goal or a prop-firm profit target.
- Compare the R of trades tagged as chasing a missed move with planned entries.
- Record whether all seven Section XIII questions were answered before entry and test the completion rate against outcomes.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB data shows how P&L visibility or profit targets change behaviour; hiding P&L is an untested practitioner heuristic.
- No concept note on goal setting or incentive design; profit-target effects appear only in the prop-firm note.
- The KB does not define what 'profitability' should mean as an objective (growth rate, drawdown-constrained return), beyond the Kelly growth criterion.

## XIV. Capital preservation and survival (the asymmetry of losses)

`philo_capital_preservation`

*Capital is the resource that buys future participation. Because even a positive-expectancy strategy meets unfavourable sequences, sizing, leverage, drawdowns and ruin must be managed so that being wrong never removes the ability to continue; losses are asymmetric, since a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover.*

**Grounded in**
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — The note gives the recovery formula 1/(1-DD) - 1 behind Section XIV's 50% to 100% example, plus streak damage under fixed-fractional risk and the gambler's-ruin approximation.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — Fixed-fractional and volatility-unit sizing are the mechanisms that keep a sequence of losses survivable.
- **Kelly Criterion & Optimal f** (`concept_kelly_criterion_optimal_f`) — Betting more than twice Kelly gives negative growth even with an edge, the formal reason over-sizing destroys future participation.
- **Leverage, Margin, Pips & Lots** (`concept_leverage_margin`) — The note calls leverage the root cause of most blown accounts and insists that risk is set by stop distance x size, not by available leverage.
- **Correlation & Portfolio Risk** (`concept_correlation_portfolio_risk`) — Correlated positions compound risk invisibly and correlations rise toward 1 in crises, so per-trade limits alone do not protect capital.
- **Prop-Firm Evaluations & Risk Rules** (`concept_prop_firm_risk_rules`) — Trailing drawdowns and daily loss limits make survival path-dependent, the clearest real-world case of capital as the licence to keep trading.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — Sizing inversely to volatility keeps the money at risk constant when markets become wilder.

**Supported by**
- **The Mathematics of Money Management** (`book_mathematics_of_money_management`) — Vince shows that leverage drives both growth and ruin, the trade-off behind Section XIV.
- **The Definitive Guide to Position Sizing** (`book_definitive_guide_position_sizing`) — Tharp's sizing strategies and R-multiple simulations are built to reach objectives while surviving drawdowns.
- **The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion** (`mathbook_kelly_capital_growth`) — The collected theory of growth-optimal betting, including fractional Kelly, formalises sizing for long-run growth and survival.
- **When Genius Failed** (`book_when_genius_failed`) — LTCM's collapse shows leverage, correlation and liquidity risk overwhelming a strong model, the failure Section XIV is written to prevent.
- **The Black Swan** (`book_black_swan`) — Taleb argues rare, high-impact events dominate outcomes and are underestimated, so sizing must allow for sequences worse than history shows.
- **Larry Hite** (`person_larry_hite`) — Hite's risk-first rule of never risking more than 1% on a trade is the practitioner form of capital preservation.
- **An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications** (`mathbook_feller_probability`) — Feller's classic treatment of ruin problems is the mathematical source of probability-of-ruin reasoning.
- **Paul Tudor Jones II** (`person_paul_tudor_jones`) — Jones's defence-first stance puts protecting capital ahead of prediction.

**Challenged by**
- **Stanley Druckenmiller — Lost Tree Club speech** (`media_druckenmiller_lost_tree_2015`) — Druckenmiller argues for heavy concentration when conviction is high, recalling Soros taking the 1992 sterling short to 200% exposure, so maximal preservation can forgo the rare outsized opportunities that drive long-run returns.
- **The Zurich Axioms** (`book_zurich_axioms`) — Gunther's axiom to always bet meaningfully warns that over-cautious stakes make even correct decisions irrelevant to wealth.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Stops do not guarantee the maximum loss in gaps and limit moves, and correlations jump in crises, so per-trade risk caps understate the real risk to capital.

**Put into practice by**
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Sizing is based on risk of ruin from win rate, R:R and average loss rather than confidence, with an average loss of about 0.5% of allocated capital, and the page notes that a 50% drawdown needs a 100% gain.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Rule 8 caps risk by trade grade as a share of the daily stop (A+ up to 80%, A 30%, B 15%, C 5%).
- **Dhesi — Liquidity Inversion Model: Sweep, FVG Inversion & LTF Confirmation** (`strategy_dhesi_liquidity_inversion`) — Two losses end the day, and when volatility widens the structural stop to 40-50 NQ points the trader cuts contracts rather than moving the stop.
- **Candace Lau — NQ Liquidity Sweep & IFVG Reversal** (`strategy_candace_lau_nq_sweep_reversal`) — The daily limit is a maximum dollar or % loss rather than a trade count, illustrated by about $3,000 on a roughly $160,000 prop account.
- **Steven Dux — Small-Cap Short Statistics Playbook** (`strategy_steven_dux_small_cap_short`) — Stop first, size second: the stop goes at real invalidation and size is reduced until the risk is acceptable, with caps of about 10% of float and 1% of volume.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It holds the recovery formula, the streak-loss formula 1 - (1-f)^k and the ruin approximation (q/p)^U used to check survivability.

**How it could be measured**
- Monte Carlo resample each setup's R-multiples at risk fractions of 0.25%, 0.5%, 1% and 2% and report the probability of reaching a 20%, 30% or 50% drawdown.
- Compare terminal wealth and maximum drawdown at each fraction with an estimated Kelly fraction shrunk for estimation error.
- Measure realised loss against planned loss per trade (slippage past stops, gaps) to quantify tail exposure.
- Track portfolio heat (total open risk) and the average correlation of open positions against realised drawdowns.
- Record drawdown depth and time to recover to check the loss asymmetry on the account's actual path.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- Most playbooks do not state risk per trade or daily limits, so the KB cannot compare preservation practices across them.
- No guidance on choosing a fraction of Kelly when the win rate is a wide-interval estimate from a discretionary journal.
- No per-instrument data on gap risk or slippage beyond stops.
- No note on the behavioural effects of drawdowns, that is, how a deep drawdown changes later decisions.

## XV. The difference between value and price

`philo_value_vs_price`

*A transaction fixes the terms of an exchange at one moment, not an objective or permanent value; participants disagree and revise their valuations. Fundamental, behavioural and statistical approaches each look for a usable relationship, and a gap between price and one's own value estimate starts an investigation rather than proving an edge, because the estimate may be wrong, the gap may persist, or costs may exceed the benefit.*

**Grounded in**
- **Auction Market Theory** (`concept_auction_market_theory`) — AMT defines value as the area where two-sided trade is accepted over time and volume and lets it migrate, a market-generated idea of value distinct from any single transaction price.
- **Volume Profile** (`concept_volume_profile`) — The value area and POC are the measurable form of where participants have agreed to trade most.
- **Mean Reversion** (`concept_mean_reversion`) — Reversion to a reference fair value works in balanced regimes but carries negative skew when the regime turns, a price-value gap that can persist and hurt.
- **Fundamental & Macro Analysis** (`concept_fundamental_macro_analysis`) — The fundamental approach estimates value from rates, growth and policy, and the note stresses that markets trade surprise versus consensus, not levels.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — EMH holds that price already reflects available information, so an apparent discrepancy is usually compensation for risk or an error in the estimate.
- **Market Sentiment & Positioning** (`concept_market_sentiment`) — Positioning and crowd beliefs show participants holding different valuations at the same price, as Section XV describes.
- **Carry Trade** (`concept_carry_trade`) — The forward-premium puzzle is a long-lived gap between a theoretical relationship and realised prices that the note explains as a crash-risk premium, a discrepancy that persists by design.

**Supported by**
- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Dalton's Market Profile treats value as what the auction accepts and separates day-timeframe traders from other-timeframe participants who move value.
- **Markets and Market Logic** (`book_markets_and_market_logic`) — Steidlmayer's original text frames the market as a self-organising auction of price, time and value.
- **Efficiently Inefficient** (`book_efficiently_inefficient`) — Pedersen argues mispricings persist because of costs and limits to arbitrage, matching Section XV's warning that a gap may persist or cost more to exploit than it pays.
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Harris catalogues participants who trade for reasons other than a view on value (hedgers, utilitarian traders), supporting that a trade price is not a shared valuation.
- **Expected Returns** (`book_expected_returns`) — Ilmanen's synthesis of value, carry and momentum premia shows that price-value gaps often reflect compensated risk rather than free edge.
- **The Most Important Thing** (`book_most_important_thing`) — Marks's second-level thinking asks what is already reflected in price, the distinction between one's own value view and the market's.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros documents gaps between perception and reality opening and closing through boom-bust sequences.

**Challenged by**
- **Reflexivity** (`concept_reflexivity`) — If biased perceptions change the fundamentals they price, there is no independent value for price to return to, which complicates the value/price distinction and Section II's claim that the market exists independently of understanding.
- **A Random Walk Down Wall Street** (`book_random_walk_down_wall_street`) — Malkiel's case for efficiency implies most perceived discrepancies are illusions and that, after costs, investigating them rarely pays.
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** (`book_reminiscences_stock_operator`) — Livermore's maxim that the market is never wrong, only opinions are, treats price as the arbiter and warns that a private value estimate invites holding losing positions.

**Put into practice by**
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Fair price is set by the event driving the market (the 9:30 ET open or the pre-news price) and dropped once value has clearly moved, and reversion is entered only after a DC or BOS confirmation.
- **Andrea Cimitan — Liquidity Auction & Order Flow Strategy** (`strategy_andrea_cimitan_amt`) — Rule 2 locates fair value with the volume profile, setups 1-2 trade failed auctions back to fair value, and setup 3 accepts a breakout as new value only when price holds outside with participation.
- **Lance Breitstein — Capitulation Mean Reversion** (`strategy_lance_breitstein_mean_reversion`) — Its scoring favours moves with no fundamental change (real news may have shifted equilibrium) and a quantifiable value anchor, and it targets only a partial retracement to the 20-period MA.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — It runs two models chosen by market state: trend continuation toward new balance, and mean reversion to the prior POC after a failed breakout.
- **Pradeep Bonde — Episodic Pivot** (`strategy_pradeep_bonde_episodic_pivot`) — An Episodic Pivot requires months of neglect, a catalyst that changes the story and rapid repricing on a gap and volume, trading the market's re-rating of value.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Its post-earnings drift model enters after a positive reaction plus an EPS beat and holds about 60 trading days, built on the slow absorption of earnings news into price.

**How it could be measured**
- For each value reference (POC, VWAP, session open, 20-period MA), measure the probability and speed of return after a deviation of k ATR, conditioned on regime and on whether news was present.
- Fit an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck half-life to deviations from fair value and check its stability across years.
- Compare reversion rates for moves with and without a fundamental catalyst, following Breitstein's criterion.
- Journal every perceived discrepancy that was investigated, traded or rejected, with its outcome net of costs.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB uses 'value' in at least three senses (auction value area, fundamental value, event-defined fair price) with no note reconciling them.
- No concept note on fundamental valuation methods for single assets, such as cash-flow or relative valuation.
- No empirical study in the KB of fair-value reversion rates by regime or after news.

## XVI. The trading journal as a record of understanding

`philo_journal`

*The journal should record the growth and testing of understanding, not just fills and P&L: conditions, reasoning, probability estimate and invalidation written before or at decision time, then a review of whether the outcome fell in the expected range, the plan was followed and emotion intervened, so that beliefs can be compared with market behaviour without hindsight reconstruction.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — The note lists the fields (setup tag and grade, regime label, rule adherence, emotion tags, R, MAE/MFE) and the daily, weekly and monthly review cadence that Section XVI describes.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Section XVI's 'what would invalidate the hypothesis' and 'was the trade executed according to plan' presuppose rules written before the trade, the subject of the trading-plan note.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The question of whether a result 'was simply one outcome within an uncertain process' is the KB's point that one trade's outcome is not feedback on the decision.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The journal's purpose of testing whether a setup deserves a 'high probability' label requires expectancy per setup, which the journal note names as the reason for setup tags.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Section XVI's example of a strategy that works under some conditions and fails under others needs a regime label on every trade, a field the journal note includes.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — Per-setup statistics (profit factor, expectancy, SQN) are the measures a journal produces when beliefs are compared with results.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — The backtesting note's warning about hindsight bias and lenient rule reading, and its fix of writing rules first and logging every instance, is the same discipline Section XVI asks of a journal.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Section XVI's question 'did emotions influence the decision?' depends on the emotion tags and adherence flags the psychology and journal notes recommend.

**Supported by**
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros's 1985 to 1986 real-time diary records his hypotheses, positions and changes of mind, a published example of a journal of understanding rather than of P&L.
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's case against judging a decision by its result supports reviewing the reasoning behind a decision separately from how it turned out.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Kahneman's account of hindsight and outcome bias explains why Section XVI insists that reasoning be recorded before or at the decision, not reconstructed later.
- **Diary of a Professional Commodity Trader** (`book_diary_professional_commodity_trader`) — Brandt runs classical charting as a business with trade logs and risk rules, a practitioner model of the written record Section XVI describes.
- **The Psychology of Trading** (`book_psychology_of_trading_steenbarger`) — Steenbarger applies structured self-observation to change trading patterns, which is the journal used as a tool for learning.
- **The Daily Trading Coach** (`book_daily_trading_coach`) — Steenbarger's self-coaching lessons rest on reviewing one's own records, supporting the journal as the basis for improvement.
- **Principles** (`book_principles_dalio`) — Dalio's practice of systematising lessons from mistakes supports turning experience into a record that can be examined instead of relying on memory.

**Challenged by**
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Slicing a journal by setup, regime, session and emotion creates many comparisons, so some slice will look strong or weak by chance; the 'differences' Section XVI hopes to discover can be multiple-testing artefacts unless the slices are fixed in advance.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb argues that looking at results too often mostly shows noise, which complicates a post-trade review of every outcome and risks reading meaning into random variation.

**Put into practice by**
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 17 journals every trade while fresh (entry, exit, stop, target, size, partials, setup type, planned or not, mistakes, emotional state, grade) and grades the process rather than the P&L.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Rules 1 to 5 log every meaningful mistake in a daily report card for a week, pick one, find its root cause with five whys and turn it into a specific, measurable playbook fix.
- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rule 10 makes a post-session journal a cool-down that records what was felt, when focus slipped and an If-Then plan, and rule 3 collects warning-sign data for weeks.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Rule 8 makes journaling mandatory at every stage, and Stage 2 collects win rate, per-setup and time-of-day statistics from the journal.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Its post-trade step logs setup tag, grade, regime, session, MAE/MFE, adherence and emotion, the fields needed to test beliefs against results.

**How it could be measured**
- Calibration: bucket the probability estimates recorded before entry and compare each bucket with its realised hit rate (Brier score, reliability diagram).
- Hindsight control: share of trades whose pre-trade fields carry a timestamp before the entry time and were never edited afterwards.
- Contradiction check: realised win rate and expectancy of trades labelled 'high probability' versus all trades, with confidence intervals.
- Condition dependence: expectancy per setup by pre-defined regime label, tested for a difference with a pre-specified interaction test rather than post-hoc slicing.
- Invalidation discipline: fraction of losing trades closed because the recorded invalidation occurred versus for other reasons.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB's journal concept note has no field for a pre-trade probability estimate or a timestamp rule against hindsight; the fuller template lives in 00_philosophy/journal-template.md, which has no node id.
- Nothing in the KB covers forecast calibration scoring (Brier score, reliability diagrams), the natural test of recorded probability estimates.
- No guidance on the minimum number of journal entries per condition before a 'performs well under X' conclusion is drawn, beyond the checklist's 30-instance rule of thumb.
- No study in the KB shows that journaling improves trading results; support is practitioner (T2) and psychology literature.

## XVII. Turning the philosophy into a scientific method

`philo_scientific_method`

*A philosophy becomes a trading method only when its claims are written as precise rules that can be found consistently in historical data, tested net of costs on data not used to build them, and examined across market conditions, with hypothesis development kept separate from evaluation so the rules do not learn the noise of the past.*

**Grounded in**
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Section XVII's steps (define the behaviour, check whether the move occurred, measure gains and losses with costs) are the systematic-backtesting checklist: clean data, realistic costs, no look-ahead, out-of-sample tests.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — The warning about adjusting rules until they fit the past is the KB's overfitting problem, with holdout, walk-forward, parameter robustness and deflated Sharpe as defences.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Whether the strategy 'would have remained profitable under realistic conditions' is a test of expectancy after costs.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Section XVII asks whether performance differs across market conditions and whether deterioration means the market changed, which is regime analysis.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — Measuring gains, losses and their variation calls on the KB's metrics (Sharpe, profit factor, max drawdown, SQN, skew).
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The method's goal is to decide whether a believed advantage is a repeatable positive-expectancy condition, the KB's definition of edge.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — The note's points that almost every pattern is an autocorrelation claim and that structural breaks require rolling or walk-forward estimates are the statistical basis of Section XVII's tests.
- **Machine Learning in Finance** (`concept_machine_learning_finance`) — Purged and embargoed cross-validation and leakage control are the KB's tools for keeping development and evaluation separate when labels overlap in time.

**Supported by**
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson argues for the scientific method and data-snooping-robust tests in technical analysis and finds most rules fail them, the same discipline Section XVII asks for.
- **Advances in Financial Machine Learning** (`book_advances_financial_ml`) — López de Prado sets out purged cross-validation and backtest-overfitting statistics that make the separation of development and evaluation precise.
- **The Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (`paper_deflated_sharpe_2014`) — The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects a backtest's Sharpe for the number of trials, skew and kurtosis, a formal answer to 'adjusting rules until they fit the past'.
- **Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems** (`book_building_winning_algo_systems`) — Davey describes a champion trader's process for developing, validating and monitoring systems, a practitioner version of Section XVII.
- **Systematic Trading** (`book_systematic_trading`) — Carver's framework for systematic rules stresses avoiding overfitting and keeping rules simple, matching 'the simplest framework that adequately measures'.
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper's falsifiability criterion is the philosophical root of 'a theory becomes a trading method only when its claims can be tested against actual outcomes'.
- **All of Statistics** (`mathbook_all_of_statistics`) — Wasserman covers inference, the bootstrap, hypothesis testing and multiple testing, the statistical tools the method needs.
- **Marcos López de Prado — The 7 Reasons Most Machine Learning Funds Fail** (`media_lopez_de_prado_7_reasons`) — The talk attributes most quant-fund failures to process errors such as backtest overfitting, independent support for separating research from evaluation.

**Challenged by**
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — If market efficiency evolves with its participants, a rule validated out of sample is tested on a population that is already changing, so passing the test cannot license confidence in the next period the way a repeatable laboratory experiment would.
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — A few years of out-of-sample data may contain no tail event, so a negatively skewed strategy can pass Section XVII's tests and still carry hidden blow-up risk.
- **Consensus & Conflicts across sources** (`synthesis_consensus_conflicts`) — It records discretionary versus systematic trading as a genuine conflict; many practitioner lenses rely on judgment that resists being defined 'clearly enough to identify it consistently in historical data'.

**Put into practice by**
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rules 3 and 6 to 8 define entry, exit and size with no room for interpretation, tune only on an 80% in-sample split, freeze the rules before testing the unseen 20%, then run Monte Carlo shuffle and resampling.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 7 tries to break each strategy with parameter-robustness, in-sample/out-of-sample periods spanning different regimes, Monte Carlo drawdown and cross-market tests, and rule 2 caps entries at two or three rules to limit curve fitting.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 2 checks expected move per trade against fees, spread and slippage, and rules 4 to 7 tell the trader to research the exact thresholds and time exits for each market.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — The playbook backtests the account model by its evaluation pass rate under the firm's real rules and suggests about 50 runs to reduce small-sample noise.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — Its L8 block gives the expected best Sharpe from N skill-less trials, the deflated Sharpe ratio and profit factor, the arithmetic of Section XVII's tests.
- **The Unified Trading Intelligence Framework** (`synthesis_unified_framework`) — Its L8 stage compares live with backtest, deflates Sharpe for the number of trials and uses walk-forward testing.

**How it could be measured**
- Out-of-sample to in-sample ratio of expectancy and Sharpe for rules frozen before the holdout was viewed.
- Deflated Sharpe ratio or probability of backtest overfitting computed with the actual number of variants tried.
- Parameter-sensitivity surface: whether performance forms a plateau or a spike across neighbouring parameter values.
- Break-even cost per side and net expectancy under 1x, 2x and 3x the assumed costs.
- Performance by regime bucket using cut-offs fixed on in-sample data only.
- Whether live drawdowns and losing streaks stay within the Monte Carlo percentile bands of the backtest.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB note explains how to log the number of research trials so that deflated Sharpe or PBO can be computed; the evidence lab does this but has no node id.
- Most Chart Fanatics playbooks leave key terms undefined (for example 'displacement', 'balanced', 'expanded' volatility), so their rules cannot yet be coded and tested.
- Cost and slippage assumptions are missing even from the two systematic frameworks (Matteo Conti and Naoufel Taief list them as unspecified).
- The concept notes give the standard error of a win rate but no statistical-power guidance on how many trades a test needs.

## XVIII. The practical doctrine (the 12 principles)

`philo_practical_doctrine`

*The philosophy is condensed into twelve working principles: understand before acting, read movements in context, treat the future as uncertain, use probability conditionally, demand mathematical justification, do not force trades, respond to new evidence methodically, separate decision quality from outcomes, protect capital and emotional independence, record and investigate, and put the process before money.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — A doctrine of principles is put into force as rules written before the trade, the trading-plan concept.
- **Multi-Timeframe Analysis** (`concept_multi_timeframe_analysis`) — Principle 2's reading of a short-term move within its broader context is the KB's top-down, multi-timeframe analysis.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Principles 2 and 4 make the meaning of a pattern depend on market conditions, the regime concept.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Principle 5 asks for the distribution of outcomes, expected value and costs, the expectancy note's content.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Principles 3 and 8 (destination as hypothesis, losses not proof of bad decisions) restate the probabilistic-thinking note.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — Principle 9 is a sizing rule: size so unfavourable sequences cannot end the trader's ability to continue.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Principles 6, 10 and 12 guard against forcing trades, emotional override and the pull of money, the psychology note's guardrails.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Principle 11 names the journal as the instrument by which accumulated evidence improves decisions.

**Supported by**
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's seven principles of consistency (identify edges objectively, predefine and accept risk, act without hesitation, monitor errors) are an independent doctrine with the same core.
- **Market Wizards** (`book_market_wizards`) — Schwager's interviews find that top traders use very different methods but share risk control and discipline, supporting a doctrine of principles rather than a single setup.
- **Trading for a Living** (`book_trading_for_a_living`) — Elder's three Ms (mind, method, money) and fixed per-trade and monthly risk caps parallel the doctrine's split into understanding, mathematics, capital and psychology.
- **Hedge Fund Market Wizards** (`book_hedge_fund_market_wizards`) — Schwager's 40 lessons from hedge-fund managers on process, risk and adaptation independently support principles 7, 9 and 11.
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's separation of decision quality from outcome independently argues Principle 8.
- **Way of the Turtle** (`book_way_of_the_turtle`) — The Turtle experiment showed that a simple, written rule set can be taught, and that most failures came from not following it, supporting doctrine plus disciplined execution.

**Challenged by**
- **The Zurich Axioms** (`book_zurich_axioms`) — Gunther's rival twelve axioms (bet meaningfully, take profits early) conflict with Principle 9's survival-first sizing and with Principle 5's expectancy logic, which shows that compact doctrines can disagree and must be tested rather than adopted.
- **Stanley Druckenmiller — Lost Tree Club speech** (`media_druckenmiller_lost_tree_2015`) — Druckenmiller argues for concentration over diversification and recounts Soros doubling the 1992 sterling short, a successful practice that pulls against uniformly small, survival-first sizing.
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp's claim that entries are overrated and that exits and sizing drive results (his random-entry experiments) questions Principle 1's priority on understanding before acting.

**Put into practice by**
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Its ten scored lines (edge, regime, HTF bias, invalidation, size, state of mind, with fewer than 7 of 10 meaning no trade) put most of the twelve principles into one gate.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — Its rule that a trade needs market state, location and aggression in order, and that the trader stays flat if any is missing, plus 0.25 to 0.5% risk per trade, enacts Principles 2, 6 and 9.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Rule 8, the Three-Loss Rule (three failed reversions in one session stop reversions for that session), is a concrete instance of Principle 7's methodical response to changed evidence.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Rule 1's foundational principles (the goal is a repeatable process, control what you can, no single green month proves anything) and rule 8's mandatory journaling express Principles 8, 11 and 12.

**How it could be measured**
- Twelve adherence flags per trade in the journal, and the R distribution of fully adherent trades versus trades with any violation.
- Dose-response: relation between the number of principles violated on a trade and its R outcome.
- Logged no-trade decisions when the three elements disagreed, and the forward outcome of the skipped setups.
- Frequency of each violation type over time, to find which principles are hardest to keep.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB does not translate the principles into numeric thresholds (what risk fraction satisfies Principle 9, what sample satisfies Principle 5).
- Principles 2 and 7 need an operational test of 'the structure has changed'; the playbooks that come closest leave it unspecified (for example Fabio Valentini's balance test).
- There is no evidence beyond practitioner testimony that following such a rule set improves results.

## XIX. What this philosophy still needs to prove

`philo_open_questions`

*Internal coherence is not profitability: the philosophy still has to show that some behaviours are repeatable, that conditions can be measured consistently, that probability estimates are reliable, that edges survive costs and changing conditions, and that the trader can execute consistently, and it must say what evidence would prove it wrong.*

**Grounded in**
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — All six questions ask whether a believed advantage is a real, repeatable positive-expectancy condition after costs.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — The worry that 'some patterns appear by chance' is the multiple-testing problem at the centre of the overfitting note.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Questions 2 and 5 are about defining market conditions and about whether a relationship survives when they change.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Question 4 asks whether expectancy stays positive after spreads, commissions, slippage and financing.
- **Optimal Execution & Market Impact** (`concept_optimal_execution_market_impact`) — The costs listed in Question 4 (slippage, execution conditions) are the subject of the market-impact and execution-cost note.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — Whether any behaviour is genuinely repeatable is the efficient-markets debate, resolved in the KB through the adaptive-markets view.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Question 6, whether the trader can execute consistently, depends on the discipline and emotional factors in the psychology note.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — The answers have to come from research and testing, the backtesting note's domain.

**Supported by**
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper's falsifiability underlies Section XIX's demand that a serious philosophy state what evidence would show it is wrong.
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson's finding that most technical rules fail rigorous testing supports treating repeatability as an open question rather than a premise.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb shows how survivorship and luck masquerade as skill, independent support for 'some patterns appear by chance'.
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo's argument that edges appear and decay as participants adapt supports Question 5 on changing market conditions.
- **Efficiently Inefficient** (`book_efficiently_inefficient`) — Pedersen ties the existence of hedge-fund strategies to costs and limits to arbitrage, supporting Question 4's focus on whether an edge survives costs.
- **The Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (`paper_deflated_sharpe_2014`) — Bailey and López de Prado quantify how many trials inflate apparent performance, supporting Question 3's doubt about historical success rates.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros's use of his own fallibility as a method (form a hypothesis, act on it, look for what is wrong) is the same posture as keeping the philosophy open to investigation.

**Challenged by**
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Under fat tails some of these questions cannot be answered to useful precision within one trader's sample, so 'establishing what evidence would demonstrate it is wrong' may need more data than is available.
- **Unknown Market Wizards** (`book_unknown_market_wizards`) — Schwager's traders with exceptional audited records show edges in many idiosyncratic forms, often developed through live practice, which suggests some real edges may not reduce to the measurable, repeatable behaviours Questions 1 and 2 look for.

**Put into practice by**
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 2 requires an explanation of why each edge should exist (so the trader notices when it stops applying), and rule 11 monitors live results against Monte Carlo ranges.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Its approach of generating many strategies and trading only those that survive deliberate attempts to break them (rule 7) treats every strategy as a hypothesis that must earn its place.
- **Source Quality Tiers** (`framework_source_tiers`) — Its rules of evidence (win-rate claims need a sample size and an R:R; backtests are hypotheses until checked out of sample) set the evidential bar the six questions require.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It provides the standard error of a win rate, the per-trade cost formula and the deflated Sharpe ratio, the tools for Questions 3, 4 and 1.
- **Consensus & Conflicts across sources** (`synthesis_consensus_conflicts`) — Its table of claims that do not survive scrutiny (for example a 90% win rate at 1:20 R:R) shows the KB already applying Section XIX's scepticism to practitioner claims.

**How it could be measured**
- Q1: out-of-sample persistence of a conditional frequency against a null adjusted for multiple testing.
- Q2: agreement between two independent labellings of the same market condition.
- Q3: width of confidence intervals and calibration of probability estimates out of sample.
- Q4: net expectancy and break-even cost after measured spreads and slippage.
- Q5: performance across regime buckets and sub-periods, with structural-break tests.
- Q6: live versus backtest gap split into missed signals, slippage and rule violations.
- For each hypothesis, a pre-registered falsification criterion and whether it was met.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No strategy note in the KB states explicit falsification criteria.
- There is no live-trading dataset in the KB; the evidence lab's own summary says Questions 1, 2, 5 and 6 remain mostly open, with only a handful of markets and two periods tested.
- No measured transaction-cost data exists for any instrument or session.

## XX. The research behind the philosophy

`philo_research_foundations`

*Five research fields support and test the philosophy: market microstructure (how interaction produces price), probability and statistical inference (what history can establish), behavioural finance (as a source of hypotheses, not stories), backtest-overfitting research (why random patterns look convincing) and risk management (how sizing and drawdowns govern survival).*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — Section XX.1 names microstructure (order flow, liquidity, information, participant behaviour) as the field behind 'price as the interaction of participants'.
- **Liquidity** (`concept_liquidity`) — Liquidity is one of the mechanisms Section XX.1 lists as shaping the prices traders observe.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Section XX.2 lists probability distributions, variance and statistical dependence, the content of the distributions and fat-tails note.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — Section XX.3's behavioural finance (overconfidence, loss aversion, the disposition effect) is the behavioural root of the psychology note.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Section XX.4's backtest-overfitting research is the subject of the overfitting and edge-validation note.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Section XX.4 warns that a strategy can look profitable in historical data without a real advantage, the central caution of the backtesting note.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — Section XX.5 ties risk management to drawdowns and the ability to survive unfavourable outcomes, the risk-of-ruin note's subject.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — Section XX.5 names position sizing and leverage as the levers of survival.

**Supported by**

## Principle 1: Understand before acting

`principle_01_understand_before_acting`

*Price movement alone is not a reason to trade. Before entering, the trader needs a defensible explanation of current conditions.*

**Grounded in**
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The note's first component of an edge is a cause (who is on the other side, and why), the 'defensible explanation' this principle asks for.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Understanding current conditions starts with knowing what kind of market this is.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — A plan written before the trade (regime filter, precise setups) is the practical form of understanding before acting.
- **Price Action Trading** (`concept_price_action`) — Its rules (location before pattern, context decides, a trigger is not a signal) rule out acting on a bar's movement alone.
- **Breakout Trading** (`concept_breakout_trading`) — The fakeout problem (most breakouts in ranges fail) is the cost of entering only because price is moving.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — FOMO and over-trading are the emotional pull to act on movement that this principle resists.

**Supported by**
- **The Most Important Thing** (`book_most_important_thing`) — Marks's second-level thinking asks what others already believe and what is priced in before acting, a deeper form of understanding first.

- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Dalton ties tactics to the day type and the state of the auction, so the market must be read before a trade is chosen.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Acting on movement is a fast, intuitive System 1 response, and Kahneman shows when slower System 2 reasoning is needed.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's principles of consistency begin with identifying an edge objectively and defining the risk before entry.

**Challenged by**
- **Trend Following** (`concept_trend_following`) — Trend followers enter precisely because price is moving (N-day breakouts) and follow price rather than predictions, yet the knowledge base counts time-series momentum among the best-supported anomalies.
- **The Man Who Solved the Market** (`book_man_who_solved_the_market`) — Renaissance is described as trading small statistical edges at scale, which raises the question whether a causal explanation is needed when the statistical evidence is strong.

**Put into practice by**

**How it could be measured**

## Principle 3: Treat the market's future as uncertain

`principle_03_future_is_uncertain`

*A likely destination is a hypothesis, not a promise. Before acting, the trader must state the evidence for it and the conditions that would invalidate it.*

**Grounded in**
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Douglas's 'anything can happen', and expressing views as probabilities with sizes, are the mindset of this principle.
- **Trade Management** (`concept_trade_management`) — Structural stops beyond the level that invalidates the idea turn the invalidation condition into an order.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — The note says a planned R:R is a hypothesis, and the realised R depends on how often the target is reached before the stop.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Rules written before the trade (setup, stop, target) are where the evidence and the invalidation get defined.
- **Reflexivity** (`concept_reflexivity`) — The note's fallibilism (assume the thesis is flawed and look for the flaw) is the attitude this principle asks for.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — Sizing from the stop distance fixes the cost of being wrong before entry.

**Supported by**
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's first truth is that anything can happen, and his principles require the risk to be defined before each trade.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros's trading diary records his hypotheses and changes of mind; his method is to form a hypothesis, act on it, and look for what is wrong with it.
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper's falsifiability asks what observation would refute a theory, which is the role of the invalidation condition.
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke treats beliefs as bets with probabilities, not certainties.
- **The Black Swan** (`book_black_swan`) — Taleb shows that rare, high-impact events are systematically underestimated, a reason to treat any destination as uncertain.
- **Diary of a Professional Commodity Trader** (`book_diary_professional_commodity_trader`) — Brandt runs classical charting with risk defined in advance and accepts that most signals fail.

**Challenged by**
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Gaps and limit moves mean a stop does not guarantee the maximum loss, so defining the invalidation condition does not cap the cost of being wrong.
- **Liquidity** (`concept_liquidity`) — Stops cluster just beyond obvious invalidation levels and get swept, so the 'correct' structural invalidation point is the one most likely to be hit before the hypothesis plays out.

**Put into practice by**
- **Steven Dux — Small-Cap Short Statistics Playbook** (`strategy_steven_dux_small_cap_short`) — Stop first, size second: the stop goes at the real invalidation point, size is cut to fit it, and a first-red-day short is never sized as if the top were guaranteed.
- **Tori Trades — Trendline Trading Playbook: Action Line and Safety Line** (`strategy_tori_trendline_4h`) — Every trade has a Safety Line defined at entry, and a break trade without a clear opposing Safety Line must wait until one forms.
- **Ted Zhang — Stage Analysis Strategy** (`strategy_ted_zhang_stage_analysis`) — Rule 5 sets the invalidation before entry: below the base, or below the level whose loss proves the breakout failed.
- **Traveling Trader — Universal Trading Strategy** (`strategy_traveling_trader_universal`) — Rule 6 puts the stop beyond the level that proves the story wrong and rules out wide stops chosen out of fear.
- **Marci Silfrain — Measured Move Trend Strategy: Little RZY** (`strategy_marci_silfrain_measured_move`) — Rule 10 lists what invalidates the setup: a close beyond the trendline, an over-extended or irregular structure, or a collapse with no pullback.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Item 7 requires the stop to sit where the idea is wrong, not at an arbitrary distance.

**How it could be measured**
- The share of journal trades with evidence and invalidation written down before entry.
- The distribution of realised losses beyond the planned stop (gaps, slippage), to measure the fat-tail challenge.
- How often price sweeps the planned invalidation level and then reaches the target (the stop-run rate).
- A comparison of structural stops and ATR-based stops on MAE and expectancy.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The knowledge base has no data on stop slippage or gap losses.
- No study measures how often structural invalidation levels are swept before the target is reached.
- formula_sheet has no barrier-probability formula (the chance the target is reached before the stop), although triple-barrier labelling is mentioned.

## Principle 4: Use probability conditionally

`principle_04_conditional_probability`

*Estimate probabilities only for clearly defined situations, and never assume a pattern has the same implications under every market condition.*

**Grounded in**

- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Douglas's third truth ties the distribution of wins and losses to a given set of variables that define an edge.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Expectancy per setup and per regime is the quantity being conditioned.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — The Hurst exponent, variance ratio and structural-break tests are the statistical tools for defining and checking conditions.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — The volatility regime and volatility clustering are among the most measurable conditions.
- **Trading Sessions & Kill Zones** (`concept_trading_sessions_killzones`) — Time of day is a condition many playbooks use to define where an estimate applies.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Logging regime, session and setup tags for each trade produces conditional statistics.

**Supported by**
- **Introduction to Probability** (`mathbook_blitzstein_intro_probability`) — Conditioning is the central idea of the text: probabilities change with the information they are conditioned on.
- **Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** (`mathbook_jaynes_probability_logic`) — Jaynes treats probability as always relative to stated information.
- **Time Series Analysis** (`mathbook_hamilton_time_series`) — Regime-switching models formalise outcome probabilities that depend on an unobserved market state.
- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Different day types call for different tactics: the same price behaviour read differently by condition.
- **Reading Price Charts Bar by Bar** (`book_reading_price_charts_bar_by_bar`) — Brooks's observation that breakouts mostly fail in ranges and reversals mostly fail in trends is conditional probability in practice.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas defines an edge as a probability tied to the set of variables that define it.

**Challenged by**

- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo's adaptive markets imply that conditional tendencies appear, get crowded and decay, so a well-defined condition may not keep its probability.

**Put into practice by**
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — The market state (balance or imbalance) chooses the model, each model has its own session (New York for trend, London for mean reversion), and the trader stays flat if state, location or aggression is missing.
- **Tanja Trades — AMD Futures Model: Trade the Distribution Leg** (`strategy_tanja_amd_futures`) — Rule 1 classifies each day as high or low probability from the economic calendar; the day before CPI, FOMC or NFP, for example, is low probability.
- **Dylan O’Neil — VIX Futures Strategy: VIX Pressure as Confirmation for ES and NQ** (`strategy_dylan_oneil_vix_futures`) — The same ES break of the prior-day low reads as continuation when the VIX confirms and as a likely failure when it does not, and rule 8 says a rising VIX near all-time highs is not a reliable signal on its own.
- **Anthony Crudele — Futures Trend Trading Strategy: Bollinger Band Environments** (`strategy_anthony_crudele_futures_trend`) — Its Bollinger Band environments (consolidation, expansion, mean reversion) each set a different posture and a different set of allowed trades.
- **TG Capital — London Session Trident Strategy** (`strategy_tg_capital_trident`) — If the 5/9/13/21 EMAs cross or tangle the setup is invalid, so the pattern counts only when the market is trending.
- **Trader Yush — Trading Trapped Buyers & Sellers with Order Flow** (`strategy_yush_order_flow`) — Rule 2 classifies the market as balanced or imbalanced and uses a different model for each (fades at the value edges vs pullbacks into LVNs).

**How it could be measured**
- The evidence-lab S2 design: conditional cells compared with the baseline in sample and checked out of sample.
- Expectancy per setup by regime label from the journal, with confidence intervals and a minimum count per cell.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**

- There is no multiple-testing tool for conditional cells beyond the deflated Sharpe ratio.

## Principle 5: Require mathematical justification

`principle_05_mathematical_justification`

*A compelling explanation is not a demonstrated advantage. Before acting, the trader examines the full distribution of outcomes, expected value after costs, and the uncertainty around every estimate.*

**Grounded in**
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — E[R] = p*W - (1-p)*L is the core calculation the principle requires, and the note shows high win rates with negative expectancy.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The KB defines edge as positive expectancy after costs, a property of a distribution and never of a single trade.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — Sharpe, profit factor, SQN, skew and kurtosis describe the distribution beyond its mean, including hidden tail risk.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Out-of-sample tests, walk-forward and the deflated Sharpe ratio guard against a backtest that justifies a trade only by chance.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Clean data, realistic costs, no look-ahead and Monte Carlo resampling are the procedures that produce the numbers the principle demands.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Skewed, fat-tailed outcome distributions are why the principle asks for the whole distribution and not just averages.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — The breakeven win rate for a given R:R is the first mathematical check on any setup or marketing claim.

**Supported by**
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson applies data-snooping corrections and finds most technical rules fail rigorous tests, the reason explanations need mathematical support.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's point that a strategy can win often and still lose money (frequency versus magnitude) is the case for examining the distribution.
- **All of Statistics** (`mathbook_all_of_statistics`) — Wasserman covers inference, the bootstrap and multiple testing, the tools for putting uncertainty around an expected value.
- **The Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (`paper_deflated_sharpe_2014`) — Bailey and Lopez de Prado correct the Sharpe ratio for the number of trials and non-normal returns, measuring how much justification a backtest really provides.
- **Advances in Financial Machine Learning** (`book_advances_financial_ml`) — Lopez de Prado's backtest-overfitting statistics and purged cross-validation set a standard for demonstrating an advantage on financial data.
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp's R-multiples, expectancy and SQN turn a trading idea into a measurable distribution.

**Challenged by**
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Taleb shows the law of large numbers works slowly under fat tails, so an expectancy 'justified' from a few hundred trades can be false precision.
- **Brando Elite — Options Swing Trading on HTF Levels + News Catalysts** (`strategy_brando_options_swing`) — The note observes the setup appears only a few times a year, so any win rate rests on a tiny sample and rare high-conviction opportunities cannot be justified statistically before acting.
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — If edges appear and decay with the ecology of participants, an expectancy measured on past data may not apply now, however rigorous the calculation.

**Put into practice by**
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — E[R], the breakeven win rate, the standard error of a win rate, SQN and the deflated Sharpe ratio are the formulas the principle calls for.
- **Lance Breitstein — Capitulation Mean Reversion** (`strategy_lance_breitstein_mean_reversion`) — Every trade is judged on EV = win rate x reward - loss probability x risk, and rule 8 scales size to setup quality.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rules 5-8 backtest the exact rules, develop on 80% of history, freeze, test on the unseen 20%, and run shuffle and resampling Monte Carlo.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 6's baseline filters (return-to-drawdown of at least 4, profit factor of at least 1.3-1.5, two or more trades a month) are followed by rule 7's parameter-robustness, out-of-sample, Monte Carlo and cross-market tests.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 2 requires the expected move per trade to be large relative to costs, with the example of 0.2% round-trip costs taking half of a 0.4% edge.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Its risk section measures the prop evaluation pass rate under real rules rather than raw P&L and suggests about 50 evaluation backtests to reduce small-sample noise.

**How it could be measured**
- For each playbook, compute EV per trade in R after costs with a bootstrap confidence interval and the probability that EV > 0.
- Check that the Wilson lower bound of the win rate exceeds the cost-adjusted breakeven win rate.
- Compute the deflated Sharpe ratio given the number of variants tried.
- Report skew, kurtosis and the share of total R that comes from the best 5% of trades.
- Report the ratio of out-of-sample to in-sample expectancy.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No shared dataset of trade outcomes for the 47 playbooks; most cannot be checked because win rate or R:R is missing.
- No standard cost assumptions per market.
- The pre-trade checklist's 30-trade minimum is a heuristic that is not justified for fat-tailed, skewed R distributions.

## Principle 6: Do not force opportunities

`principle_06_do_not_force`

*When the evidence is unclear or one's own framework cannot justify a trade, stay out of the market: waiting is better than entering for reasons the method does not support.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Precise written setup definitions and a regime on/off filter are what make 'the framework cannot justify it' checkable.
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — FOMO, revenge trading and overconfidence are the forces that push traders to force entries.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The KB treats running a setup outside its valid regime as the most common cause of failure, so a regime mismatch is a reason to stay out.
- **Trade Execution** (`concept_trade_execution`) — The checklist requires the defined confirmation to have completed, so an entry before it is a forced trade.
- **Multi-Timeframe Analysis** (`concept_multi_timeframe_analysis`) — When higher and lower timeframes conflict the setup lacks agreement, a common no-trade condition in the playbooks.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — Chasing worsens entry price and R:R, so a late entry can turn a valid setup into one the mathematics no longer supports.

**Supported by**
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** (`book_reminiscences_stock_operator`) — Livermore's losses from trading on tips and hope are the classic case of forcing trades without justification.
- **The Mental Game of Trading** (`book_mental_game_of_trading`) — Tendler treats greed and FOMO as fixable flaws that produce trades outside the method.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Overconfidence and fast heuristic judgement explain why traders believe weak setups are good enough.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's noise-versus-signal argument supports waiting until the evidence is distinguishable from randomness.

**Challenged by**
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 10 argues that waiting for confirmation gives up part of the edge, so a strict wait-until-clear rule has a cost in entry price.
- **Way of the Turtle** (`book_way_of_the_turtle`) — Turtles who skipped signals they found unattractive failed, so if 'unclear' is judged in the moment, not forcing can turn into selectively skipping valid signals.

**Put into practice by**
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — A trade needs market state, location and aggression in that order, the trader stays flat if any one is missing, and rule 4 says no aggression means no trade.
- **Abraham Perez — Nasdaq ICT & Order Flow Scalping Strategy** (`strategy_abraham_perez_ict_orderflow`) — Rule 9 lists no-trade conditions: timeframes disagree, balanced market, no liquidity taken, no order-flow confirmation, late entry, or risk that cannot be defined.
- **Tanja Trades — AMD Futures Model: Trade the Distribution Leg** (`strategy_tanja_amd_futures`) — Rule 3 says no clear draw on liquidity means no trade, and rule 6 skips the setup and never chases if price does not retrace into the FVG.
- **Marco Trades — Liquidity Grab Trading Strategy** (`strategy_marco_trades_liquidity_grab`) — Rule 2 says no liquidity built means no trade and to stand aside, and a setup that does not form inside the session window is not taken.
- **Okala — 80/20 Nasdaq Strategy: Reactions at 20 and 80 Price Endings** (`strategy_okala_80_20_nasdaq`) — Rule 6 skips any setup that cannot take a clean 10-point stop, and rule 9 filters out partial setups away from a level.
- **Ted Zhang — Stage Analysis Strategy** (`strategy_ted_zhang_stage_analysis`) — Rule 8's no-trade list includes an extended breakout the trader would be chasing and any base too messy to define invalidation.

**How it could be measured**
- Tag trades taken with one or more plan conditions missing and compare their expectancy with fully qualified trades.
- Measure entry distance from the planned level in R and its relation to outcome.
- Count trades outside the defined session window and their R.
- Shadow-log no-trade days and estimate the expected value given up by waiting.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No objective, testable definition of 'the evidence is unclear' in the KB.
- No data compares anticipation and confirmation entries on the same setups, although the KB names this as a genuine conflict.
- No record of skipped setups to estimate the cost of not forcing.

## Principle 7: Respond to changed evidence methodically

`principle_07_respond_methodically`

*When evidence changes, first manage the existing risk, then investigate the change and revise the hypothesis if warranted, avoiding both impulsive reaction and the defence of a disproven idea.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trade Management** (`concept_trade_management`) — Structural and time stops placed where the idea is wrong are the predefined way to manage risk first.
- **Market Structure** (`concept_market_structure`) — The distinction between continuation (BOS) and change of character (CHoCH) tells whether changed price action actually refutes the hypothesis.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Investigating a change often means checking whether the regime has shifted, which can flip a setup's expectancy.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — The review loop is where the investigation is recorded and the hypothesis formally revised.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Bayesian updating is the methodical alternative to both impulsive reversal and stubborn defence.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Invalidation written before entry makes the first response mechanical rather than emotional.

**Supported by**
- **Karl Popper** (`person_karl_popper`) — Popper holds hypotheses provisionally and abandons them when refuted, the stance behind not defending a disproven hypothesis.
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros acts on a hypothesis while looking for its flaw and records his changes of mind in a diary, a model of methodical revision.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Predefining and fully accepting risk removes the hesitation that turns changed evidence into an impulsive or frozen response.
- **Best Loser Wins** (`book_best_loser_wins`) — Taking small losses instantly is the 'manage existing risk' step done without drama.
- **Principles** (`book_principles_dalio`) — Dalio's practice of systematising lessons from mistakes is the investigate-and-revise half of the principle.
- **Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** (`mathbook_jaynes_probability_logic`) — Probability as extended logic gives the rule for updating belief in a hypothesis as evidence arrives.

**Challenged by**
- **Liquidity Sweep Reversal** (`concept_liquidity_sweep_reversal`) — Price often runs the stops beyond an obvious invalidation level and then reverses, so an immediate risk response can exit just before the hypothesis is vindicated.
- **Brando Elite — Options Swing Trading on HTF Levels + News Catalysts** (`strategy_brando_options_swing`) — Its approach replaces a tight stop with a position small enough to lose entirely, deliberately holding through adverse moves rather than reacting to them.

**Put into practice by**
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — After three failed reversion trades in a session, reversions stop for that session, and rule 9 drops an old fair price once value has clearly moved.
- **Fredy Sarmiento — Institutional Options Flow: Gamma Wall Reversal** (`strategy_fredy_sarmiento_options_flow`) — Rule 9 accepts a failed level: when price breaks a wall with strength and holds, the trader stops fading it and reassesses.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 12 benches a strategy only when drawdown nears its Monte Carlo maximum or underperformance persists, never on short-term losses alone.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 11 treats a breach of the Monte Carlo range as a trigger for review, not proof that the strategy is broken.
- **Andrea Cimitan — Liquidity Auction & Order Flow Strategy** (`strategy_andrea_cimitan_amt`) — Rule 9 holds while the controlling side stays in control and exits when the opposite side takes over, tying the exit to the evidence for the trade.
- **Alex Temiz — First Red Day Strategy** (`strategy_alex_temiz_first_red_day`) — Its invalidation is a reclaim of the previous day's close that holds, with an immediate exit and no holding in hope.

**How it could be measured**
- Measure the delay between an invalidation trigger and the actual exit, and its cost in R.
- Track what share of hypothesis revisions were preceded by a journaled investigation.
- Record the price path after invalidation exits to estimate how often exits fall on detours.
- Compare later expectancy of revised setups with unrevised ones.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB procedure says what evidence, and how much, is required before revising a hypothesis.
- No data on how often stops are run at structural invalidation levels.

## Principle 8: Separate decision quality from individual outcomes

`principle_08_decision_quality_not_outcome`

*A loss does not by itself mean a bad decision and a win does not by itself mean a good one; the trader evaluates the process and the result together.*

**Grounded in**
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The note's consequence that a single trade's outcome is not feedback on the decision, with Duke's warning against 'resulting', is the principle itself.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Edge belongs to the distribution of outcomes, so decision quality is judged by expected value, not by one result.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Rule-adherence and setup-grade fields record process quality separately from the R result.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — SQN and confidence-adjusted metrics judge results over a sample, where outcome evidence becomes meaningful.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — Normal losing streaks show that runs of bad outcomes are compatible with sound decisions.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — A written plan is the benchmark that defines whether a decision followed or broke the process.

**Supported by**
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's book is built on separating decision quality from outcome.
- **Annie Duke** (`person_annie_duke`) — Duke is the KB's main voice for process over outcome and the 'resulting' error.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas's random distribution of wins and losses for any edge means individual results say little about the decision.
- **Mark Douglas** (`person_mark_douglas`) — Douglas's five fundamental truths underpin the KB's probabilistic-thinking note.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's alternative histories judge a decision by the outcomes it could have produced.
- **MIT 15.S50 Poker Theory and Analytics** (`media_mit_15s50_poker`) — Poker analytics evaluates decisions under uncertainty by expected value, a setting where sound plays lose individual hands.

**Challenged by**
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** (`book_reminiscences_stock_operator`) — Livermore's view that the market is never wrong and opinions are makes the outcome the final arbiter, a warning that 'good process' can become an excuse for poor reasoning.
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Under fat tails, years of outcomes may be needed to separate skill from luck, so for a long time process evaluation rests largely on the trader's own judgment.
- **Ed Seykota** (`person_ed_seykota`) — Seykota's claim that everybody gets what they want from the market treats outcome patterns as evidence about the trader's process and intentions.

**Put into practice by**
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 17 journals mistakes, emotional state and a trade grade, and grades the process, not the P&L.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Stage 4 judges consistency by execution rather than outcome, week over week.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — The daily report card logs process mistakes without judgment, separately from results.
- **Kyle Williams — Small Cap Short Selling Playbook: First Red Day and Parabolic Short** (`strategy_kyle_williams_first_red_day`) — Its review tracks performance by setup grade, so quality and outcome are compared over a sample.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — The standard error of an estimated win rate (about +/-7% at n = 50) shows how little a short run of outcomes proves.

**How it could be measured**
- Fill a 2x2 table of process followed or not x win or loss and compare expectancy and frequency by cell.
- Score each trade's process before seeing the result and test whether process scores predict R over rolling samples.
- Compute how many trades are needed before the confidence interval on expectancy excludes zero.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB rubric for scoring decision quality; the playbooks that grade trades leave their criteria unspecified.
- No guidance on blinding the process score from the outcome to avoid hindsight bias.

## Principle 9: Protect capital

`principle_09_protect_capital`

*Position size must be set so that runs of unfavourable outcomes, which even a positive-expectancy strategy will produce, cannot needlessly threaten the trader's ability to keep participating.*

**Grounded in**
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — The principle is a sizing rule; the note's fixed-fractional and volatility-unit models and 'size down in drawdowns' are its tools.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — Streak damage 1 - (1 - f)^k, recovery asymmetry and the gambler's-ruin approximation are the mathematics of why size governs survival.
- **Kelly Criterion & Optimal f** (`concept_kelly_criterion_optimal_f`) — Kelly gives the growth-optimal fraction and shows that betting more than about twice Kelly produces negative growth even with an edge, an upper bound for 'protect capital'.
- **Leverage, Margin, Pips & Lots** (`concept_leverage_margin`) — The note's rule that stop distance times size, not leverage, sets risk is the mechanism by which capital is protected.
- **Correlation & Portfolio Risk** (`concept_correlation_portfolio_risk`) — Several correlated positions at small risk each are one larger bet, so protecting capital requires a portfolio-heat cap as well as a per-trade cap.
- **Prop-Firm Evaluations & Risk Rules** (`concept_prop_firm_risk_rules`) — Treating the drawdown limit as the bankroll and using daily loss limits are concrete forms of sizing so unfavourable sequences do not end the account.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The principle exists because a positive expectancy only pays off over many trades, which requires surviving the losing streaks along the way.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — Sizing inversely to volatility (the Turtle N unit) keeps the money at risk constant as market conditions change.

**Supported by**
- **The Definitive Guide to Position Sizing** (`book_definitive_guide_position_sizing`) — Tharp's position-sizing strategies, SQN and R-multiple simulation show how size, not entry, decides whether objectives are met without ruin.
- **The Mathematics of Money Management** (`book_mathematics_of_money_management`) — Vince shows that leverage drives both growth and ruin, the formal basis for sizing to survive.
- **A New Interpretation of Information Rate** (`paper_kelly_1956`) — Kelly's growth-optimal betting fraction is the original mathematical link between edge, bet size and long-run survival.
- **The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion** (`mathbook_kelly_capital_growth`) — The collected work on fractional Kelly explains why practitioners bet a fraction of the growth-optimal size to cut drawdowns.
- **An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications** (`mathbook_feller_probability`) — Feller's classic treatment of ruin problems is the probability theory behind 'unfavourable sequences can end participation'.
- **Larry Hite** (`person_larry_hite`) — Hite's risk-first rule of never risking more than about 1% on a trade is a practitioner statement of the principle.
- **Paul Tudor Jones II** (`person_paul_tudor_jones`) — Jones's emphasis on defence first and sizing down when trading poorly (documented in Market Wizards) independently argue capital protection.
- **When Genius Failed** (`book_when_genius_failed`) — LTCM's collapse shows leverage, correlation and liquidity risk overwhelming a strong model, a case study in what the principle prevents.

**Challenged by**
- **Stanley Druckenmiller — Lost Tree Club speech** (`media_druckenmiller_lost_tree_2015`) — Druckenmiller's argument for concentration and Soros doubling the sterling short show that some of the largest documented gains came from sizing far above survival-first norms when conviction was high.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Stops do not guarantee the maximum loss in gaps and limit moves, and correlations rise toward one in crises, so sizing from stop distance can understate how much a single sequence can take.
- **The Zurich Axioms** (`book_zurich_axioms`) — Gunther's axiom to always bet meaningfully argues that very small fractions protect capital at the cost of never compounding it, a tension the principle does not address.

**Put into practice by**
- **Ariel Hernandez — Real Simple Swing Setups: EP, HVC, Flat Base, U&R, High Tight Flag** (`strategy_ariel_hernandez_real_simple`) — Rule 8 sizes each position so a stopped-out trade costs only 0.5 to 1% of total equity, and rule 13 adds exposure only as trades work and the market confirms.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — Both models risk 0.25 to 0.5% of the account per trade with a stop that is never widened.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 10 allocates 5 to 25% of the account per strategy with an average loss of about 0.5% of that allocation, sizes by risk of ruin, and rule 12 cuts size when drawdown nears its Monte Carlo maximum.
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Rule 8 grades a trade before sizing it and caps risk at 80%, 30%, 15% or 5% of the daily stop for A+, A, B and C trades.
- **Dhesi — Liquidity Inversion Model: Sweep, FVG Inversion & LTF Confirmation** (`strategy_dhesi_liquidity_inversion`) — Rule 14 ends the day after two losses, a hard circuit breaker on losing sequences.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — Its L6 block gives fixed-fractional size, streak damage, recovery after drawdown and the gambler's-ruin approximation.

**How it could be measured**
- Monte Carlo shuffle and resampling of the journal's R-multiples at candidate risk fractions: probability of a 20% or 50% drawdown and of breaching a ruin threshold.
- Realised maximum drawdown and longest losing streak compared with the simulated distribution.
- Realised loss divided by planned loss per trade, to measure slippage through stops and gaps.
- Total open risk including correlated positions (portfolio heat) at each point in time.
- Chosen risk fraction compared with an estimated Kelly fraction, with the estimate's uncertainty.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- Most Chart Fanatics playbooks leave risk per trade and daily or weekly limits 'not specified' (for example Gala Trades, Marci Silfrain, Clement Ang, Jared Tendler), so the KB has few concrete T2 sizing rules to test.
- There is no KB data on how often and by how much stops are gapped through by instrument.
- No KB guidance on shrinking size to reflect uncertainty in the edge estimate, beyond the fractional-Kelly heuristic.
- Portfolio-level drawdown caps across several strategies are not specified anywhere (the Naoufel Taief note lists this as missing).

## Principle 10: Protect emotional independence

`principle_10_emotional_independence`

*Fear, greed, frustration and the urge to win back losses must not override the tested process, and the method should be revised only through evidence from disciplined investigation, never through emotional impulse.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Psychology & Discipline** (`concept_trading_psychology`) — The note covers loss aversion, revenge trading, FOMO and tilt, the emotions the principle names, and lists operational guardrails against them.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Thinking in distributions removes the emotional weight of a single loss, which is Douglas's route to executing without fear.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Psychology rules written into the plan (maximum trades, a break after N losses, no revenge trades) make the process the decision-maker.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Emotion tags and rule-adherence flags in the journal are how emotional interference is detected and separated from method problems.
- **Position Sizing** (`concept_position_sizing`) — Oversized positions amplify emotion, so sizing is a practical lever for emotional independence.
- **Risk of Ruin & Drawdown Math** (`concept_risk_of_ruin_drawdown`) — Pre-set daily loss limits and size cuts after a drawdown stop frustration from turning losses into a demand for recovery.

**Supported by**
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas argues that fear is a misperception of risk and that a loss accepted before entry leaves nothing to fear in the moment.
- **The Disciplined Trader** (`book_disciplined_trader`) — Douglas's earlier book argues that mental frameworks about risk and being wrong are the bottleneck, not analysis.
- **The Mental Game of Trading** (`book_mental_game_of_trading`) — Tendler diagnoses greed, fear, tilt and discipline problems and fixes their underlying flaws, a method for the principle.
- **Best Loser Wins** (`book_best_loser_wins`) — Hougaard argues that the edge is behavioural and that traders must train against instinct, cutting losses instantly and without drama.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** (`book_thinking_fast_slow`) — Kahneman's loss aversion and prospect theory explain why traders hold losers and cut winners, the bias the principle is meant to override.
- **The Daily Trading Coach** (`book_daily_trading_coach`) — Steenbarger's self-coaching lessons give structured ways to keep emotional states from steering decisions.

**Challenged by**
- **The Hour Between Dog and Wolf** (`book_hour_between_dog_wolf`) — Coates finds that hormones such as testosterone and cortisol drive cycles of risk-taking, so emotional states are partly physiological and may need external limits rather than willpower alone.
- **Prop-Firm Evaluations & Risk Rules** (`concept_prop_firm_risk_rules`) — Profit targets, trailing drawdowns and deadlines in the prop-firm environment most KB traders use push toward over-aggression, creating the emotional pressure the principle tries to remove.

**Put into practice by**
- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rule 8's mid-session reset (name the emotion, move, refocus, then at most one small trade or a 15-minute break, with the session over if that limit is broken) and rule 9's emotional expected value are direct procedures for the principle.
- **Dhesi — Liquidity Inversion Model: Sweep, FVG Inversion & LTF Confirmation** (`strategy_dhesi_liquidity_inversion`) — Rule 14 ends the day after two losses, which the note links to stopping revenge trading.
- **Clement Ang — Price Cycle Strategy: Continuation Base and Late-Stage Failed Base** (`strategy_clement_ang_price_cycle`) — Rule 27 allows about three attempts per ticker and then stops trading the name, an explicit tilt control.
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 13 hides the running P&L and manages from the chart, and the comfort test treats a loss you would not be at ease taking as a sign of oversizing.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Its rule that emotions taking over means the size is too big turns emotional interference into a sizing signal.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Item 10 requires no revenge, no FOMO and no tilt, and that the trade's loss be accepted in advance.

**How it could be measured**
- Expectancy and rule adherence of trades tagged calm versus trades tagged fear, FOMO, frustration or revenge in the journal.
- Trade frequency and size in the trades after a loss or a losing streak compared with baseline, as a revenge-trading signature.
- Disposition-effect test: holding time of losers versus winners relative to the plan, and early exits before target.
- Rule changes made within a few days of a drawdown versus changes made at a scheduled review.
- Compliance rate with session-stop rules.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No KB data links emotion tags to performance; the psychology frameworks are practitioner heuristics (Tendler's 60/80/100% scale has no scoring method).
- The KB has no operational test of when a method change is evidence-based rather than impulsive (for example a minimum sample or review cadence before a rule changes).
- Behavioural-finance papers on the disposition effect and overtrading are not linkable nodes in the index.

## Principle 11: Record and investigate

`principle_11_record_and_investigate`

*Keep a journal of hypotheses, decisions, outcomes and changes in understanding, and let the accumulated record, not memory, improve future decisions.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — The principle names the journal; the note supplies its fields and its daily, weekly and monthly review cadence, including an edge-decay check.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — Accumulated evidence becomes useful through per-setup metrics such as expectancy, profit factor and SQN.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The record is what lets expectancy be estimated per setup rather than assumed.
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — Investigating why results differ requires a regime label on each record.
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — Recorded decisions are judged against the written plan, and changes in understanding become plan revisions.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Comparing live records with backtest expectations is how the journal detects decay or execution problems.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Letting evidence accumulate over many trades, rather than reacting to one, is the probabilistic stance the principle requires.

**Supported by**
- **The Alchemy of Finance** (`book_alchemy_of_finance`) — Soros's real-time diary of hypotheses and changes of mind is a published record-and-investigate practice.
- **Diary of a Professional Commodity Trader** (`book_diary_professional_commodity_trader`) — Brandt's trade logs and the analysis of why most signals fail show a professional record used to investigate a method.
- **Principles** (`book_principles_dalio`) — Dalio's systematic recording of mistakes and lessons is an independent case for turning experience into examinable records.
- **The Daily Trading Coach** (`book_daily_trading_coach`) — Steenbarger's self-coaching is built on reviewing one's own trading records.
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke argues for reviewing decisions in a way that separates skill from luck, which needs a record of the reasoning at the time.
- **Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems** (`book_building_winning_algo_systems`) — Davey's process includes monitoring live systems against their development results, record-and-investigate applied to systems.

**Challenged by**
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Mining the journal for patterns after the fact is itself a multiple-testing exercise, so 'accumulated evidence' can produce false lessons unless the questions are fixed in advance.
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — If edges evolve with the market's ecology, older records describe conditions that may no longer exist, so a longer record is not automatically better evidence for the next decision.

**Put into practice by**
- **Jeff Holden — Momentum Model Performance Development** (`strategy_jeff_holden_momentum_model`) — Its development loop logs mistakes for a week, picks the most important one, finds the root cause with five whys, writes a measurable fix and treats friction as the next data point.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Stage 2 collects win rate, per-setup and time-of-day data with daily review, and Stage 4 uses journal data only for small refinements while tracking week-over-week consistency.
- **Kyle Williams — Small Cap Short Selling Playbook: First Red Day and Parabolic Short** (`strategy_kyle_williams_first_red_day`) — Its review rule tracks performance by setup grade.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 11 monitors live results against Monte Carlo ranges and treats a breach as a reason to review, not as automatic proof the strategy is broken.
- **The Unified Trading Intelligence Framework** (`synthesis_unified_framework`) — Its L8 stage journals per setup, regime and session and compares live results with the backtest.

**How it could be measured**
- Journal completeness: share of trades, and of no-trade decisions, with every pre-trade field filled.
- Learning curve: rolling expectancy and adherence rate before and after each documented rule change (interrupted time series).
- Frequency of a targeted mistake before and after a root-cause fix.
- Monthly gap between live and backtest expectancy per setup.
- Hypothesis log: number of hypotheses revised or retired, and the evidence that triggered each change.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB gives no statistical method for journal review (minimum sample per slice, control for multiple comparisons).
- There is no standard taxonomy of trading mistakes to make records comparable over time.
- The full journal template in 00_philosophy has no node id, so the principle cannot be linked to it in the graph.

## Principle 12: Follow the process, not the promise of money

`principle_12_process_not_money`

*The pull of profit must never replace checking whether a trade is justified: understanding, evidence, a tested model, probabilities, the cost of being wrong and fit with the process come first, and the money is judged only afterwards.*

**Grounded in**
- **Trading Plan** (`concept_trading_plan`) — 'Does this decision follow my established process?' presupposes a written plan.
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — Judging the process rather than a single trade's payoff is the probabilistic-thinking stance against 'resulting'.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — Money is properly evaluated as expectancy over many trades, not as what one trade might pay.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The principle says to follow the process that has demonstrated an advantage, which is an edge in the KB's sense.
- **Risk:Reward & R-Multiples** (`concept_risk_reward_r_multiples`) — Expressing outcomes in R rather than currency is a practical way to take money out of the decision.

**Supported by**
- **Thinking in Bets** (`book_thinking_in_bets`) — Duke's warning against judging decisions by their outcomes is the independent argument for process before money.
- **Trading in the Zone** (`book_trading_in_the_zone`) — Douglas argues that consistency comes from executing a defined edge without fixation on any single trade's result.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb shows that profits can come from luck, so money cannot by itself show that a decision was justified.
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** (`book_trade_your_way_financial_freedom`) — Tharp's R-multiples measure every trade in units of initial risk, shifting attention from money to the system's expectancy.
- **Way of the Turtle** (`book_way_of_the_turtle`) — Faith reports that Turtles who failed usually did so by not taking every signal, showing that departures from process, not the rules, destroyed results.

**Challenged by**
- **Prop-Firm Evaluations & Risk Rules** (`concept_prop_firm_risk_rules`) — Prop-firm economics put money targets, payouts and consistency rules at the centre of most KB traders' environment; JJ Simon's playbook even sets stops and targets from the account model rather than chart invalidation, which makes money-first decisions structural.

**Put into practice by**
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Rule 1 states that the goal is not to make a dollar figure but to build a repeatable process, with money as a by-product.
- **Gala Trades — Price Action Strategy: Hourly Levels with Breaking Retest, Bounce & Rejection** (`strategy_gala_trades_price_action`) — Rule 13 hides the running P&L and manages from the chart, and rule 17 grades the process rather than the P&L.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 9 runs strategies with no discretionary overrides and rule 12 forbids pulling a strategy for short-term underperformance, a 'process over prediction' design.
- **Kris Verma — Small-Cap Shorting: Gap Fades, Backside Parabolics & Halt Exhaustion** (`strategy_kris_verma_shorting`) — Setup B states that the goal is consistency, not catching every move, and the method is judged by overall profit factor rather than single trades.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — None of its ten gates concerns the profit amount; a trade proceeds only if at least seven are met.

**How it could be measured**
- Share of trades that were unplanned or below the checklist threshold, and their expectancy versus planned trades.
- Size and frequency changes after large wins or losses, as a money-salience effect.
- Session outcomes with the P&L display hidden versus visible.
- Days on which trading continued to reach a money target, and the R of trades taken after the target pressure began.
- Forward outcomes of setups skipped because the process did not justify them.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB has no evidence on how payout or profit-target incentives change trader behaviour.
- Research on overtrading and the cost of trading activity (for example Barber and Odean) is not a linkable node in the index.
- No KB comparison exists between process grades and P&L for any strategy.

## Which market behaviours are genuinely repeatable?

`question_repeatable_behaviors`

*Which market behaviours are genuinely repeatable, given that some patterns in historical data arise by chance and reflect no lasting relationship?*

**Grounded in**
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — A repeatable behaviour that pays after costs is the KB's definition of an edge.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — The note sets out the efficient-markets null and the counter-evidence (momentum, short-horizon reversal, variance-ratio tests) that bears on repeatability.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Separating repeatable behaviour from chance patterns is the multiple-testing problem.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — Almost every pattern claim is a claim about autocorrelation structure, which these tools test.
- **Trend Following** (`concept_trend_following`) — Time-series momentum, documented across a long history of futures data, is one of the KB's strongest candidates for a repeatable behaviour.
- **Mean Reversion** (`concept_mean_reversion`) — Short-horizon mean reversion is the other main candidate the KB lists against the random-walk null.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Repeatability can only be established by testing defined rules on history and then on new data.

**Supported by**
- **A Non-Random Walk Down Wall Street** (`book_non_random_walk`) — Lo and MacKinlay's variance-ratio tests reject the random walk and find predictable components, evidence that some behaviours recur.
- **Evidence-Based Technical Analysis** (`book_evidence_based_ta`) — Aronson shows that most technical rules fail rigorous, data-snooping-aware testing, so repeatability must be demonstrated rather than assumed.
- **Expected Returns** (`book_expected_returns`) — Ilmanen synthesises the evidence on value, carry, momentum and volatility premia, the most studied candidates for repeatable behaviour.
- **Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns** (`book_encyclopedia_chart_patterns`) — Bulkowski reports statistics on chart-pattern failure rates and average moves, a measured rather than anecdotal view of repeatability.
- **The Econometrics of Financial Markets** (`mathbook_campbell_lo_mackinlay`) — The econometrics text covers formal tests of return predictability, the tools for this question.
- **Cliff Asness** (`person_cliff_asness`) — Asness's factor and time-series momentum research is a body of evidence on behaviours that have persisted across markets.
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo argues that edges appear, get crowded and decay, so repeatability is real but conditional and temporary.

**Challenged by**
- **A Random Walk Down Wall Street** (`book_random_walk_down_wall_street`) — Malkiel's case for efficiency implies that for most traders and horizons the honest answer may be that almost no visible pattern is exploitable.
- **Reflexivity** (`concept_reflexivity`) — If participants' beliefs and actions change the market, a behaviour's repeatability is not fixed: discovering and trading it can alter or end it.

**Put into practice by**
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — It treats four candidate behaviours (opening-range breakout, VWAP trend, post-earnings drift, overnight anomaly) as hypotheses tied to a stated cause and tested under the same out-of-sample protocol.
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — It generates thousands of rule sets and keeps only the few that survive robustness tests across parameters, regimes, markets and timeframes.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — It restricts the search to three directional behaviours (short-term momentum, mean reversion, long-term momentum) and tells the trader to research each market's thresholds.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — The expected best Sharpe from N skill-less trials and the Hurst exponent give quantitative tests of chance versus structure.

**How it could be measured**
- Out-of-sample persistence of a defined behaviour's conditional frequency, tested against a null corrected for multiple testing.
- Variance-ratio, autocorrelation and Hurst estimates by horizon and market.
- Replication rate of a behaviour across markets and non-overlapping periods.
- Placebo test: the same rule on shuffled or simulated random-walk data, to estimate how often it 'works' by chance.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB has no catalogue of independently replicated market behaviours with effect sizes.
- Practitioner playbooks rarely give a sample size or win rate, so their behaviours cannot be checked for repeatability.
- The evidence lab found only 1 of 11 in-sample conditional differences holding out of sample, on a handful of markets; broader evidence is missing.

## How should the relevant conditions be defined and measured?

`question_define_conditions`

*How can the conditions a probability estimate depends on (volatility, trend, liquidity and others) be defined so they are measurable and identified the same way every time?*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The regime note's trend/range by high/low volatility grid and its detectors are the KB's starting vocabulary for conditions.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — Realised volatility, ATR and implied volatility are the measurable forms of the volatility condition Section XIX names.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — The Hurst exponent, variance ratio and structural-break tests are quantitative regime diagnostics.
- **Auction Market Theory** (`concept_auction_market_theory`) — Balance versus imbalance is a condition definition used by several playbooks.
- **Market Structure** (`concept_market_structure`) — Clean higher highs and higher lows versus overlapping swings is the structural definition of trend versus range.
- **Trading Sessions & Kill Zones** (`concept_trading_sessions_killzones`) — Time of day is the most easily measured condition many playbooks rely on.
- **Machine Learning in Finance** (`concept_machine_learning_finance`) — Labelling and feature construction (including structural-break features) are the formal way to turn conditions into data.

**Supported by**
- **Time Series Analysis** (`mathbook_hamilton_time_series`) — Hamilton's regime-switching models are the standard statistical way to define and estimate market states.
- **Analysis of Financial Time Series** (`mathbook_tsay_financial_time_series`) — Tsay's GARCH and volatility models make the volatility condition measurable and forecastable.
- **Trading Systems and Methods** (`book_trading_systems_and_methods`) — Kaufman's reference includes measures such as the efficiency ratio and testing methods for regime filters.
- **Mind Over Markets** (`book_mind_over_markets`) — Dalton defines day types and balance versus imbalance, a discretionary but explicit taxonomy of conditions.
- **New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems** (`book_new_concepts_technical_trading_systems`) — Wilder introduced ATR and ADX, two of the most used numeric measures of volatility and trend strength.
- **Advances in Financial Machine Learning** (`book_advances_financial_ml`) — López de Prado covers structural-break features and event sampling, ways to define conditions in data.

**Challenged by**
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Every extra condition splits the sample further and adds another way to overfit, so more precise conditions can make estimates less reliable, not more.

**Put into practice by**
- **TG Capital — London Session Trident Strategy** (`strategy_tg_capital_trident`) — Rule 2 requires the 5, 9, 13 (or 15) and 21 EMAs to be clearly stacked in the trade direction and marks the setup invalid if they cross or tangle, a numeric regime gate.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — Market state (balanced or out of balance) is the first gate and selects which of two models applies.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Rule 8's Three-Loss Rule uses three failed reversions in a session as an observable sign that the market is trending and setting a new fair value.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rules 4 and 6 define 'normal' movement and 'abnormal' deviation numerically (average daily range versus a 2x expansion; usual distance from a 5-day SMA versus a much larger gap).
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It gives the Hurst exponent and Wilder's ATR formula as measurable regime inputs.

**How it could be measured**
- Agreement (for example Cohen's kappa) between two people, or the same person on two dates, labelling the same charts.
- Stability of labels under small parameter changes (for example an ADX threshold of 20 versus 25).
- Real-time versus hindsight labels: classify with frozen cut-offs as the data arrive and compare with labels assigned later.
- Observations per condition cell, against a minimum sample.
- Whether the label changes the out-of-sample distribution of outcomes (for example a two-sample KS test).

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- Many playbooks list the key condition as unspecified, for example an objective test for balance (Fabio Valentini), what a healthy market is (Ariel Hernandez) and expanded versus dead volatility (Dhesi).
- The KB has no standard regime taxonomy with numeric thresholds shared across notes.
- There is no guidance on testing the reliability of discretionary condition labels.

## How reliable are the probability estimates?

`question_estimate_reliability`

*How reliable are probability estimates when a historical success rate may rest on too few observations or on conditions that will not recur?*

**Grounded in**
- **Probabilistic Thinking** (`concept_probabilistic_thinking`) — The note warns that a win rate estimated from 30 trades has a wide confidence interval and should be treated as a belief to update.
- **Probability, Distributions & Fat Tails** (`concept_probability_fat_tails`) — Sample means and variances converge slowly under fat tails, and the note recommends Bayesian updating of edge estimates.
- **Performance Metrics** (`concept_performance_metrics`) — SQN is essentially a t-statistic of the edge, a direct measure of how distinguishable the estimate is from zero.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The quantity whose reliability matters is expectancy, which combines win rate and payoff estimates.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Selection among many variants biases the chosen estimate upward, a main source of unreliability.
- **Kelly Criterion & Optimal f** (`concept_kelly_criterion_optimal_f`) — The Kelly note shows that overestimating the edge leads to overbetting, so estimate reliability feeds directly into sizing.

**Supported by**
- **All of Statistics** (`mathbook_all_of_statistics`) — Wasserman covers confidence intervals and the bootstrap, the tools for stating how reliable an estimate is.
- **Statistical Inference** (`mathbook_casella_berger`) — Casella and Berger give the theory of estimation and its uncertainty.
- **Statistical Consequences of Fat Tails** (`mathbook_statistical_consequences_fat_tails`) — Taleb shows that under fat tails the law of large numbers works slowly, so historical frequencies are less reliable than standard formulas suggest.
- **The Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (`paper_deflated_sharpe_2014`) — The deflated Sharpe ratio quantifies how selection from many trials inflates an estimate.
- **Fooled by Randomness** (`book_fooled_by_randomness`) — Taleb's survivorship-bias argument explains why a good historical record can be a poor guide to future frequency.

**Challenged by**
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — If the market's ecology keeps changing, there may be no stable underlying probability for a large sample to converge on, so reliability is limited by non-stationarity, not only by sample size.

**Put into practice by**
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It gives the standard error of an estimated win rate (about plus or minus 7 points at n = 50 and p = 0.5) and SQN as a t-statistic.
- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — Item 1 requires at least 30 logged instances with positive expectancy after costs before a setup counts as an edge.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — It suggests about 50 evaluation backtests to reduce small-sample noise in the pass-rate estimate.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 8 resamples trades with replacement to estimate the range of expected return, drawdown, longest losing streak and the chance of a given loss.

**How it could be measured**
- Confidence or credible intervals on win rate and expectancy per setup, and the sample needed for a target precision or power.
- Calibration of stated probabilities against realised frequencies on new data (Brier score).
- Selection haircut: the gap between in-sample estimates of chosen rules and their out-of-sample results.
- Bootstrap probability that expectancy is zero or below.
- Stability of the estimate across non-overlapping sub-periods.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB has no calibration tools (Brier score, reliability diagrams) for checking probability estimates.
- No concept note turns an edge size into the number of trades needed to detect it; the evidence lab's power calculation has no node id.
- Practitioner win-rate claims rarely state a sample size, so their reliability cannot be assessed.

## Does the advantage survive trading costs?

`question_survives_costs`

*Do spreads, commissions, slippage, financing and execution conditions leave a positive expectancy, or do they turn an apparently profitable strategy into a losing one?*

**Grounded in**
- **Optimal Execution & Market Impact** (`concept_optimal_execution_market_impact`) — The note covers implementation shortfall, temporary and permanent impact and the square-root law, the mechanics of execution cost.
- **Trade Execution** (`concept_trade_execution`) — It lists spread, commission, slippage against intended price and missed fills as the costs to log.
- **Market Microstructure** (`concept_market_microstructure`) — The bid-ask spread and order types that create costs are microstructure.
- **Scalping** (`concept_scalping`) — The scalping note says costs are a large share of each target, making cost survival the deciding test for short-horizon edges.
- **Expectancy** (`concept_expectancy`) — The question is whether expectancy stays positive once costs are subtracted, as the philosophy's EV formula requires.
- **Backtesting** (`concept_backtesting`) — Realistic costs, especially slippage on stops and around news, are one of the four requirements of a systematic backtest.

**Supported by**
- **Trading and Exchanges** (`book_trading_and_exchanges`) — Harris explains the components of the spread, how transaction costs are measured and why short-term trading is close to zero-sum after costs.
- **Trades, Quotes and Prices** (`mathbook_bouchaud_trades_quotes_prices`) — Bouchaud and co-authors document the square-root impact law, which says how costs grow with order size.
- **Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions** (`paper_almgren_chriss_2000`) — Almgren and Chriss model the trade-off between impact cost and price risk, the foundation of execution-cost analysis.
- **Optimal Trading Strategies** (`mathbook_kissell_optimal_trading`) — Kissell and Glantz cover transaction-cost analysis, the measurement this question needs.
- **Inside the Black Box** (`book_inside_the_black_box`) — Narang treats a transaction-cost model as one of the core parts of any quant strategy.
- **Algorithmic Trading and DMA** (`mathbook_johnson_algorithmic_dma`) — Johnson explains execution algorithms and order routing, the practical levers on cost.
- **Flash Boys** (`book_flash_boys`) — Lewis describes how market structure and latency can disadvantage slower orders, a cost retail traders rarely measure.

**Challenged by**
- **Efficiently Inefficient** (`book_efficiently_inefficient`) — Pedersen argues that inefficiencies persist because costs and limits to arbitrage deter others, so costs are not only an obstacle but the reason some edges exist, which makes the question one of relative cost advantage.

**Put into practice by**
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 2 requires the expected move per trade to be large next to fees, spread and slippage (its example: about 0.2% round-trip costs take half of a 0.4% edge), and rule 1 avoids the most competitive high-liquidity, low-timeframe mix.
- **Kris Verma — Small-Cap Shorting: Gap Fades, Backside Parabolics & Halt Exhaustion** (`strategy_kris_verma_shorting`) — Its risk section counts hard-to-borrow and locate fees as costs that eat profit and reuses located shares through recycling to reduce them.
- **Core Formulas of Analysis & Execution** (`formula_sheet`) — It gives per-trade friction as half the spread plus commission plus slippage, and the square-root impact formula.
- **Consensus & Conflicts across sources** (`synthesis_consensus_conflicts`) — It resolves the high-R:R versus high-win-rate conflict with the rule that only expectancy after costs matters.

**How it could be measured**
- Break-even cost per side: the cost at which net expectancy reaches zero, compared with measured spread plus commission.
- Implementation shortfall per trade from the journal: fill price versus decision price, and the distribution of slippage on stops.
- Net expectancy with confidence intervals by market and timeframe, and the ratio of cost to gross edge.
- Sensitivity of out-of-sample results to 1x, 2x and 3x the assumed costs.
- Turnover times cost per trade compared with gross expectancy per period.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB has no measured spread or slippage data by instrument or session.
- Both systematic frameworks (Matteo Conti, Naoufel Taief) leave cost and slippage assumptions unspecified, and the evidence lab uses flat cost assumptions.
- Financing and borrow costs appear only in the Kris Verma note; overnight financing for leveraged FX and futures is not covered.

## Does the strategy survive changing market conditions?

`question_survives_regime_change`

*Does a relationship that worked in one period keep working when market conditions change, or does it weaken or disappear?*

**Grounded in**
- **Market Regimes** (`concept_market_regimes`) — The note says the same setup can have opposite expectancy in different regimes, which is exactly this question.
- **Random Walk & Efficient Market Hypothesis** (`concept_random_walk_efficient_markets`) — Its adaptive-markets synthesis says edges appear, get crowded and decay, a mechanism for relationships weakening.
- **Time-Series Statistics** (`concept_time_series_statistics`) — Structural-break tests and rolling estimates are the statistical way to detect when a relationship has changed.
- **Edge** (`concept_edge`) — The edge note warns that edges decay as they are crowded and must be monitored.
- **Volatility** (`concept_volatility`) — Volatility clustering and regime shifts change stop distances, targets and the behaviour of setups.
- **Overfitting & Edge Validation** (`concept_overfitting_validation`) — Walk-forward testing evaluates a strategy only on forward segments, a direct test of survival across periods.
- **Trading Journal & Performance Review** (`concept_trading_journal_review`) — Its monthly or quarterly edge-decay check against backtest expectations is the live monitor for this question.

**Supported by**
- **Adaptive Markets** (`book_adaptive_markets`) — Lo's hypothesis that efficiency changes with the ecology of participants explains why relationships weaken over time.
- **Time Series Analysis** (`mathbook_hamilton_time_series`) — Hamilton's regime-switching models give a formal treatment of relationships that change between states.
- **Mastering the Market Cycle** (`book_mastering_market_cycle`) — Marks shows how economic, credit and psychological cycles change the environment in which any strategy operates.
- **When Genius Failed** (`book_when_genius_failed`) — LTCM's models, built on one set of conditions, failed when correlations and liquidity changed, a case study of failure under regime change.
- **Systematic Trading** (`book_systematic_trading`) — Carver's emphasis on robust, simple rules and avoiding overfitting is aimed at strategies that survive changing conditions.
- **Building Winning Algorithmic Trading Systems** (`book_building_winning_algo_systems`) — Davey's development process includes monitoring live systems for deterioration after deployment.

**Challenged by**
- **Trend Following** (`book_trend_following_covel`) — Covel's case that price-based trend following with strict risk control is durable and even crisis-resilient suggests some slow, diversified relationships do survive regime change, so the answer may differ sharply by strategy type.
- **The Black Swan** (`book_black_swan`) — If regime breaks are rare, high-impact events that are systematically underestimated, survival can often be judged only after the fact, which limits how far the question can be answered in advance.

**Put into practice by**
- **Naoufel Taief — No-Code Algorithmic Strategy Portfolio: Generate, Stress-Test & Rotate** (`strategy_naoufel_taief_algorithmic`) — Rule 7 requires in-sample and out-of-sample periods to include different regimes (bull, bear, high and low volatility), and rules 11 to 13 keep an incubation pool, bench strategies near their expected maximum drawdown and replace them.
- **Matteo Conti — Institutional Strategy Development Framework** (`strategy_matteo_conti_institutional`) — Rule 2 ties each model to a cause so the trader can notice when it stops applying, and rule 11 reviews a strategy when live results leave the Monte Carlo range.
- **JJ Simon — Fair Pricing Theory: Continuation & Reversion** (`strategy_jj_simon_fair_pricing`) — Rules 8 and 9 stop reversion trades after three failures in a session and drop an old fair price once value has clearly moved.
- **Pavel — First-Principles Trading Framework** (`strategy_pavel_first_principles`) — Rule 12 treats research as continuous because edges decay as more capital trades them, fastest on lower timeframes.
- **Fabio Valentini — Auction Market Theory + Order-Flow Scalping** (`strategy_fabio_valentini_amt_order_flow`) — It runs separate trend and mean-reversion models selected by market state, so a regime change switches the model instead of breaking it.
- **Umar Ashraf — 5-Stage Trader Development Framework** (`strategy_umar_ashraf_5_stage`) — Stage 5 tracks performance across market conditions so the trader can adapt.

**How it could be measured**
- Rolling-window expectancy of the strategy's R series with structural-break tests (Chow, CUSUM).
- Performance by regime bucket with cut-offs frozen in sample, and whether the sign of expectancy holds in each bucket.
- Walk-forward efficiency: out-of-sample over in-sample performance per window.
- Share of time live drawdown spends outside the Monte Carlo percentile bands.
- Performance before and after a behaviour became widely published.

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- No Chart Fanatics strategy in the KB has a multi-regime test record.
- There is no standard decay metric or benching threshold in the KB; the Naoufel Taief note itself leaves 'extended underperformance' undefined.
- The adaptive-markets and post-publication-decay papers are not linkable nodes in the index; only Lo's book is.

- **Jared Tendler — Trading Psychology Playbook: Emotion, Performance & the Mental Game** (`strategy_jared_tendler_psychology`) — Rules 5 to 8 set a pre-session state check with one process goal, note emotional shifts during the session and use a reset with a hard session-ending limit.

- **Master Pre-Trade Checklist** (`synthesis_pre_trade_checklist`) — A scored checklist applied before every trade standardises the decision so it is made the same way each time.

**How it could be measured**
- Signal capture rate: share of valid setups (found by replaying the written rules) that were actually taken live.
- Deviation from plan: entry and exit slippage against plan, stop moves and early exits per trade.
- Live versus backtest expectancy gap split into missed trades, slippage and rule violations.
- Variance of risk per trade against the planned risk.
- Behaviour after losses compared with baseline (trade frequency, size, setup grade).

**Gaps in the knowledge base**
- The KB has no data on any trader's live-versus-backtest execution gap.
- There is no KB definition or threshold for 'consistent' execution.
- No KB evidence compares discretionary and automated execution of the same rules.
